# Badshah Store — Decisions (ADRs)

Short records of why each key decision was made. Where a decision overrides PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS.md, that is noted. This file records reasons; `docs/architecture.md` is the source of truth for the current design.

## D1. Modular monolith, one Django deployable app

- **Decision:** Single Django project with apps under `apps/`; no microservices in MVP.
- **Why:** One delivery origin, one store, simpler ops and deployments. Integrations remain behind interfaces so a later FastAPI service or shipping provider can be swapped without touching core logic.

## D2. Business logic in `services.py`, thin views

- **Decision:** Put core logic (cart, checkout, stock, orders, payments, coupons) in per-app `services.py`; keep views as organizers of requests/responses.
- **Why:** Makes logic testable without the HTTP layer, keeps reusable by a future API, and localises the risky money/stock paths for review.

## D3. Money is Decimal INR, never float

- **Decision:** All monetary fields are `DecimalField`; INR with 2 decimals; no float in pricing, totals, taxes, or gateway amounts except at the gateway boundary with exact conversion and errors on fractional paise.
- **Why:** Avoids classic floating-point money bugs; GST-inclusive display and invoice math must be exact.

## D4. Timezone and locale are Django settings

- **Decision:** `TIME_ZONE = Asia/Kolkata`, `USE_TZ = True`, `LANGUAGE_CODE = en-IN` (with i18n scaffolding), and Indian digit-grouping formatting in templates/utils. These are not stored in `ShopSettings`.
- **Why:** Shops operate on IST; mixing store-level timezone into business data causes subtle bugs. Locale formatting is a presentation concern.

## D5. Inventory as an immutable ledger

- **Decision:** `InventoryMovement` is the source of truth for stock; `on_hand` is derived from movements; stock reservation separate from on-hand.
- **Why:** Allows full audit trail (receive, sale, return, damage, adjustment), reconciliation against physical counts, and correct async reservation expiry without corrupting stock.

## D6. Reservation model split by kind and expiry

- **Decision:** `StockReservation` with `ACTIVE/CONSUMED/RELEASED` and kinds `CHECKOUT_PENDING_PAYMENT` and `HELD_FOR_FULFILMENT`. Expiry (15 min, configurable) applies only to unpaid online checkouts; COD/paid orders hold until packing/cancellation/delivery.
- **Why:** Prevents holding stock for abandoned carts forever while keeping COD/paid orders stable until fulfilment. Separates "customer may still abandon" from "we are fulfilling".

## D7. Sale movement written once, at fulfilment, with reservation consumption

- **Decision:** Only one service function writes `SALE` movements, in the same transaction that consumes the matching reservation at packing.
- **Why:** Eliminates double-deduction between reservation and sale; centralises the one place stock leaves inventory for sales.

## D8. No negative stock from sales; explicit adjustment only

- **Decision:** Sales check availability and never create negative `on_hand`; only an explicit adjustment with reason may create negative `on_hand`, logged with a warning.
- **Why:** Negative stock from races or bugs is dangerous for an inventory-ledger model; make it an explicit, auditable exception, not a side effect.

## D9. Payment captured after reservation expiry

- **Decision:** If payment is captured after the reservation expired, try to re-reserve; if stock is gone, auto-refund and notify the customer.
- **Why:** Razorpay async/payment-completion timing can exceed checkout reservation; we must not silently sell unavailable stock or leave the order in an inconsistent state.

## D10. Order state machine centralized

- **Decision:** Explicit statuses with a single enforced transition path; `OrderStatusLog` for every transition with timestamp and actor. COD skips payment states.
- **Why:** Many people touch orders in the admin; a single source of truth for allowed transitions prevents illegal states and makes audit/reversal reasoning clear.

## D11. Substitutions preserve the original line

- **Decision:** Keep the original line with status `SUBSTITUTED` and add a new line linked by `replaces`; recompute price/GST at substitute's current selling price; customer picks `substitute / refund / call me` at checkout.
- **Why:** Keeps an accurate record of what the customer ordered vs what was fulfilled, and what price/GST actually applied; supports refunds and disputes.

## D12. Order finalization at packing

- **Decision:** At end of packing, finalise: cancel unfulfilled quantities, release reservations, make refund/adjustment as needed.
- **Why:** Prevents dangling reservations and unfulfilled lines from lingering; makes the stock and order state consistent when a packer finishes.

## D13. One order = one invoice

- **Decision:** Each order has exactly one invoice generated at packing/dispatch from fulfilled quantities.
- **Why:** GST invoicing for a provision shop should map cleanly to one sale per customer order; avoids splitting tax invoices in confusing ways.

## D14. Numbering: locked counter, financial year label, gapless sequential

- **Decision:** `NumberSeriesCounter` keyed by `(prefix, financial_year)`, incremented under `select_for_update` in a locked transaction; no reset job. Financial-year label from April start. Order numbers `BS/26-27/000001`; invoice numbers `INV/26-27/000001`, max 16 chars (GST rule, to be confirmed by CA), sequential and gapless.
- **Why:** Locking prevents duplicate numbers under concurrent order creation; financial-year key matches Indian accounting practice; gapless sequential invoices are expected for GST records. CA to confirm the 16-char rule and exact prefix style.

## D15. Webhook idempotency via unique event_id

- **Decision:** Verify Razorpay signature first, then insert `WebhookEvent` (unique `event_id`) in one transaction; on conflict return 200; process and mark processed. Out-of-order events handled only through allowed transitions.
- **Why:** Duplicate webhooks must never double-charge, double-ship, or double-decrement stock; idempotent insert is the gate. Webhook is the source of truth, not the browser redirect.

## D16. Coupon concurrency safety

- **Decision:** Unique constraint on `(coupon, order)` plus `select_for_update` on the coupon row while checking limits and redeeming.
- **Why:** Prevents overselling a coupon's usage cap under concurrent checkouts.

## D17. OTP stored as HMAC, not plaintext

- **Decision:** Store an HMAC over phone + purpose + code using a server secret; constant-time comparison; short expiry; limited verify attempts; per-phone rate limits.
- **Why:** Reduces blast radius if DB is compromised; still allows verification. Note: this protects stored representation, but OTP delivery itself remains a provider/transport concern. Never log OTP codes or message bodies, including in `NotificationLog`.

## D18. Authorization on every object access

- **Decision:** Customer can only see their own orders/addresses; staff access governed by role/permissions; no client-supplied IDs assumed owned.
- **Why:** Basic multi-tenant correctness for a customer-facing app.

## D19. Delivery fee and free-delivery threshold live on DeliveryZone

- **Decision:** `DeliveryZone` carries the fee and free-delivery threshold. `PinCodeZone` only links pin codes to a zone.
- **Why:** Different pin codes can have different economics; keeping fee rules on the zone avoids one global fee that doesn't match reality, and `PinCodeZone` stays a simple membership link.

## D20. Slot capacity computed live under lock

- **Decision:** Capacity computed on the fly inside the order-placement transaction with a lock; no cached `orders_count`.
- **Why:** Cached counts drift under concurrency; computing live with a lock keeps slot limits correct.

## D21. COD cap applied at checkout total, not re-enforced after substitutions

- **Decision:** COD capped at ₹2,000 per order, checked on the order total at checkout only.
- **Why:** Matches the business rule as stated; substitutions recompute the total but the cap decision is made at checkout. This is a deliberate scope choice; review if policy changes.

## D22. Reservation transition on payment capture or COD placement

- **Decision:** When an online payment is captured, or a COD order is placed, the reservation changes from `CHECKOUT_PENDING_PAYMENT` to `HELD_FOR_FULFILMENT` inside the same transaction as the order status change.
- **Why:** Keeps the reservation state and order state in lockstep; the checkout reservation is short-lived and may expire, while the fulfilment reservation is held until packing/cancellation/delivery. Doing the transition in the same transaction avoids a split-state window.

## D23. Quantities are whole-number integers for now

- **Decision:** Order/cart quantities are integers. No loose or weight-based items yet; revisit if you sell items by weight.
- **Why:** Keeps the MVP data model and stock math simple and correct. Weight-based or loose quantities would need a different unit/pricing model.
- **Update:** Narrowed by D42. Packet variants stay whole-number; variants sold loose by weight use decimal quantities.

## D24. No guest checkout; OTP login required at checkout

- **Decision:** The MVP has no guest checkout. OTP login is required before an order can be placed.
- **Why:** Matches the locked scope; simplifies authorization, order ownership, notifications, and stock reservation ownership.

## D25. Provider defaults

- **Decision:** Razorpay in test mode only until told otherwise. OTP/SMS/WhatsApp and email use console backends in development; real MSG91 and Brevo later, with MSG91 DLT registration first.
- **Why:** Lets development proceed without live credentials or regulated registrations; keeps the integration points testable with a console backend.

## D26. Search starts with PostgreSQL full-text + trigram

- **Decision:** Begin with PostgreSQL full-text and trigram similarity behind an interface; add semantic/embedding search later.
- **Why:** Adequate for typos and Hinglish spellings at MVP scale, no separate search infrastructure yet.

## D27. Media: S3-compatible in prod, local in dev

- **Decision:** S3-compatible object storage in production, local storage in development, with image resizing.
- **Why:** Keeps dev simple; prod gets durable, CDN-friendly storage.

## D28. Tailwind via pinned CDN in Phase 1; build step before launch

- **Decision:** Phase 1 loads Tailwind from a pinned CDN; HTMX and Alpine.js vendored into `static/`. Note: a Tailwind build step is required before launch.
- **Why:** Fastest path to a styled, mobile-first UI in Phase 1 without a JS toolchain yet. CDN is a placeholder for a proper build, recorded as a known debt.

## D29. Deferred items (not MVP)

- **Decision:** PWA, full Hindi i18n content, pan-India shipping, subscriptions, loyalty, and the FastAPI AI service are deferred. i18n scaffolding starts now; everything else is not built.
- **Why:** MVP scope is hyperlocal ordering + admin operations; defer the rest to avoid over-building.

## D30. Tests run against PostgreSQL 16

- **Decision:** Tests and CI target PostgreSQL 16 (Compose locally, service container in CI) because locking and constraint tests need it.
- **Why:** SQLite cannot exercise `select_for_update` and several constraint/idempotency behaviors we rely on.

## D31. Custom user model with phone as USERNAME_FIELD, set before first migration

- **Decision:** `AUTH_USER_MODEL` set to the accounts custom user before the first migration; phone normalised to E.164; empty email stored as NULL; conditional unique constraints among active users; role separate from `is_staff`; customers cannot reach admin through role.
- **Why:** Phone OTP auth requires phone as the identity field; early custom user avoids painful migrations later. Role/eligibility separation keeps customer accounts out of the admin surface by design.

## D32. Staff 2FA before launch (recommended)

- **Decision:** Recommended, not built in Phase 1: add 2FA for staff/admin accounts before launch.
- **Why:** Admin access controls stock, orders, refunds, and payments; stronger staff authentication reduces risk before go-live. Deferred to keep Phase 1 scoped, but flagged here so it is not forgotten.

## D33. Ruff rule exceptions

- **Decision:** Ruff is configured to ignore a small number of rules where the warning conflicts with Django conventions, settings layout, or intentional test data.
- **Why:** We want ruff/mypy coverage without forcing fake annotations or changes that fight Django's normal idioms. The exceptions are narrow and documented so they do not quietly widen.

### Global ignores

- `RUF012` is ignored globally.
- **Reason:** It flags mutable class attributes without `ClassVar`. That is normal Django usage for things like `Meta`, admin options, and migration class attributes, and annotating each one with `ClassVar` adds noise without real safety.

### Per-file ignores

- `S105` and `S106` are ignored in test code only (`tests/**/*.py` and `**/tests/*.py`).
- **Reason:** Test code intentionally contains hard-coded passwords, tokens, and fake secret-like strings. Treating those as real secret detections would produce constant noise without security value.
- `F405` is ignored in settings modules only (`badshahstore/settings/*.py`).
- **Reason:** Settings modules use `from .base import *` by design, so names are brought in through the star import rather than being referenced as attributed imports.

### Targeted suppression

- `DJ001` is suppressed with `# noqa: DJ001` on `User.email` only.
- **Reason:** The field is `models.EmailField("email address", null=True, blank=True)` with no `unique=True` and no `UniqueConstraint` that includes `email` in `User.Meta`. Email is optional because login uses phone (`USERNAME_FIELD = "phone"`), not email.

## D34. Pre-commit runs ruff and mypy through `uv run`

- **Decision:** `.pre-commit-config.yaml` uses `repo: local` hooks with `entry: uv run ruff check ...`, `uv run ruff format ...`, and `uv run mypy .` rather than installing duplicates of those tools into the pre-commit environment.
- **Why:** This keeps pre-commit using the exact versions and dependencies locked in `uv.lock`, so local hook runs match the versions used by developers and CI. It also avoids maintaining a second pinned copy of ruff/mypy inside `.pre-commit-config.yaml`.

## D35. Pre-commit passes `--force-exclude` to ruff

- **Decision:** The ruff pre-commit hooks use `ruff check --fix --force-exclude` and `ruff format --force-exclude`.
- **Why:** Pre-commit typically passes changed filenames explicitly, which can override ruff's normal exclude handling. `--force-exclude` makes ruff respect the excludes in `pyproject.toml` even when filenames are supplied directly, so generated code matching `apps/*/migrations` stays excluded in both pre-commit and direct `uv run` runs.

## D36. Proxy HTTPS trust is controlled only by SECURE_PROXY_SSL_HEADER

- **Decision:** `SECURE_PROXY_SSL_HEADER` is the only setting that makes Django trust a proxy's HTTPS header, and the settings read it from the environment only when it is explicitly set. The `USE_X_FORWARDED_PROTO` variable was removed from `badshahstore/settings/base.py`, `badshahstore/settings/prod.py` and `.env.example`. `USE_X_FORWARDED_HOST` is unchanged.
- **Why:** `USE_X_FORWARDED_PROTO` is not a Django setting, so it had no effect, but its name suggested protection that did not exist. A setting that looks like a security control and does nothing gives false assurance. `SECURE_PROXY_SSL_HEADER` should be set only behind a reverse proxy that terminates HTTPS and overwrites that header, because otherwise a client could fake it.

## D37. Django's Unicode slug check rejects Devanagari vowel signs

- **Decision:** Hindi text is not used in slugs. Hindi names go in the name fields (`name`, `name_hi`). Slugs are romanised ASCII (see D38). The rule is explained in a code comment in `apps/catalog/models.py` and checked by `test_slug_rejects_devanagari` in `apps/catalog/tests/test_models.py`.
- **Why:** Brand and Category slugs were first created with `SlugField(allow_unicode=True)` (migration `0001_initial`) so that Hindi could be used. Django's Unicode slug check accepts only letters, digits, hyphens and underscores, and Devanagari vowel signs (combining marks, such as the final "ा" in "आटा") do not count as letters, so ordinary Hindi words failed validation. Loosening the validation would have meant writing our own slug rules, so slugs were switched to ASCII instead.

## D38. Slugs are plain ASCII only; Hindi goes in name fields

- **Decision:** Brand, Category and Product slugs use Django's standard `SlugField`, which allows only ASCII letters, digits, hyphens and underscores. Migration `0002_slug_ascii_only` changed Brand and Category slugs from `allow_unicode=True` to this default. Hindi text goes in the name fields (`name`, `name_hi`). The test `test_slug_rejects_devanagari` in `apps/catalog/tests/test_models.py` enforces the rule.
- **Why:** This follows from D37: Django's Unicode slug check rejects Devanagari vowel signs, so Hindi slugs would fail validation for ordinary Hindi words. Romanised slugs (for example `chana-dal`) also stay readable and safe in URLs on every device. The admin fills the slug from the English name automatically.

## D39. Database check constraints on ProductVariant

- **Decision:** The catalog stores money and tax invariants in the database with `CheckConstraint`s on `ProductVariant`, not only in Django model validators. The current constraints are `catalog_variant_mrp_positive` (MRP > 0), `catalog_variant_selling_price_positive` (selling_price > 0), `catalog_variant_selling_price_lte_mrp` (selling_price <= MRP), `catalog_variant_cost_price_non_negative` (cost_price is null or >= 0), and `catalog_variant_gst_rate_0_to_100` (GST rate between 0 and 100 inclusive). These exist in both the model and the migration that created the variant table.
- **Why:** Some rules are cheap and dangerous if they can be bypassed, so they belong at the database level. The existing code shows that model validators already help at the form/validation layer (`hsn_code` digits, `gst_rate_percent` bounds, price checks in `full_clean`), but the database constraints are what actually stop invalid rows from being written. The test file for products checks these by expecting `IntegrityError` from the DB when a constraint is violated, and also checks `ValidationError` from `full_clean` where the model validator catches the same class of problem earlier. The migration `0003_product_and_variant.py` is the concrete evidence for the constraint names and conditions.

## D40. Pricing helpers live in `apps/catalog/services.py`

- **Decision:** Catalog pricing is in pure, database-free helper functions in `apps/catalog/services.py`, and all money uses `Decimal` INR. The main helpers are `round_money` (round to whole paise with halves going up, `ROUND_HALF_UP`), `gst_breakdown` (split a GST-inclusive price into taxable value, GST, CGST and SGST), `savings_amount`, `savings_percent` (whole-number percent, rounded **down** so it is never overstated), and `unit_price` (price per standard quantity such as per kg or per 100 g). `GstBreakdown` and `UnitPrice` are frozen dataclasses.
- **Why:** Keeping pricing logic out of models and views makes it easy to reuse for the storefront, admin, and invoices, and to test without a database. Money rounding and GST math is exactly the kind of code that is easy to get subtly wrong, so it is centralised and labelled as a review point before invoices are issued. The current code does the GST split from a GST-inclusive price, rounds the GST amount, rounds CGST, and sets SGST as the remainder so CGST + SGST always equal the GST amount exactly. Savings percent is rounded down on purpose so the shop does not overstate a discount. Some details are still CA-dependent and are flagged in code comments: the rounding rule, the exact HSN format, and the GST split for intra-state vs inter-state sales (the current code assumes intra-state CGST/SGST for Madhya Pradesh and leaves IGST for a later phase). Because the exact rounding and split rules were not independently confirmed from anything outside the code, this entry is reconstructed from `services.py` and the related tests; the owner should confirm the CA-facing details before invoices go live.

## D41. Product images: model, upload rules and storage

- **Decision:** `ProductImage` belongs to `Product`, not to `ProductVariant`. Fields: `image`, `alt_text`, `sort_order` (the lowest value is the main image; there is no `is_primary` flag) and `width`/`height`, which Django fills in automatically. Rows can be deleted outright (no `ArchivableModel`). Files are stored at `products/<product_id>/<random uuid>.<ext>`. Uploads must be JPG, PNG or WebP, at most 2 MB and 8000 pixels on the longest side, and Pillow must confirm the real file format, so a renamed file is refused (the limits are constants in `apps/catalog/validators.py`). In development files go to the local `MEDIA_ROOT`, served by Django only when `DEBUG` is on. No resizing or WebP conversion yet. Deleting an image row or a product does not delete the file from disk.
- **Why:** All variants of a product share the same photos. Using the lowest `sort_order` as the main image avoids a flag that would need its own constraint. Stored width and height let the storefront reserve space and avoid layout shift on slow phones. Random file names avoid clashes, odd characters and path tricks. Checking the real file content stops a harmless-looking file name hiding another file type. Resizing and WebP are deferred to the storefront slices (23 and 25) or Phase 6, when the needed sizes are known; this narrows the brief's "responsive images in WebP" for now. Production storage stays S3-compatible (D27) and can be switched later through Django's `STORAGES` setting.

## D42. Inventory: ledger-derived stock, loose-by-weight quantities, batch and expiry, supplier, low-stock threshold

- **Decision:**
  1. Current stock is the sum of `quantity_change` over a variant's `InventoryMovement` rows. There is no cached `StockLevel` table. The inventory services will lock the `ProductVariant` row with `select_for_update()`, then read the sum, check it and write the movement in the same transaction.
  2. Quantities are decimals with 3 places (`Decimal(12, 3)`), counted in the variant's own unit. Packet variants stay whole-number: the services reject fractions for them. A variant sold loose by weight has `is_sold_loose=True`, unit `KG` or `L`, `pack_size` 1 (so `selling_price` is the price per kg or per litre) and a `quantity_step` (for example 0.100) that limits what a customer may order. Packet variants have step 1. The step applies to cart quantities (Phase 3), not to the ledger: staff may adjust loose stock by any gram amount. This narrows D23.
  3. A product sold both loose and in packs is two variants with two separate stock pools. Repacking is two `ADJUSTMENT` movements with a reason. A `REPACK` movement type can be added later if it is frequent.
  4. `InventoryMovement` has an optional `batch_number` and `expiry_date`, allowed only on `PURCHASE_RECEIVED` rows (a database check constraint enforces this). There is no first-expiry-first-out logic.
  5. `Supplier` is a minimal optional record (name, phone, notes; archivable). A movement has an optional `supplier` foreign key with `on_delete=PROTECT`. It is not enforced in the database that only purchases carry a supplier.
  6. `ProductVariant.low_stock_threshold` is an optional non-negative decimal (same units as stock). A blank value means no alert. A variant counts as low stock when on-hand is less than or equal to the threshold.
  7. `quantity_change` is never 0. Database constraints: `PURCHASE_RECEIVED` and `RETURN` are positive; `IN_STORE_SALE`, `SALE` and `DAMAGE` are negative; `ADJUSTMENT` may be either sign but needs a reason. Movement rows are never updated or deleted. `created_by` is nullable (imports and system actions).
- **Why:** At about 100 SKUs a sum over indexed rows is fast, and one source of truth cannot drift, so a cache is added only if it proves slow. Locking the variant row gives the services a lock target even though there is no stock row. Some products are sold loose by weight, and changing the quantity type after carts and orders exist would touch much more code, so decimals are decided now, before any cart or order exists. Keeping packets and loose items as separate variants keeps stock math and pricing simple. Expiry tracking is per received lot only: the expiry view can say that a lot is near expiry while the variant still has stock, but it cannot say how many units of that lot remain, because sales are not tied to lots. A supplier record and a low-stock threshold are the simplest forms that meet the brief. Signed quantities with per-type sign rules in the database stop a wrongly signed movement from being written.

## D43. Inventory services: single write path, locking, quantity rules, negative stock, integrity check

- **Decision:**
  1. The functions in `apps/inventory/services.py` are the only code that writes `InventoryMovement` rows, through one private function `_record_movement`. Admin actions, imports and other apps call the services and never insert movements directly.
  2. Every write runs inside `transaction.atomic()`, locks the `ProductVariant` row with `select_for_update()` (reloading it, so its flags are current), then reads the sum, checks the rules and inserts the movement. One variant per call. Work that touches several variants must lock them in ascending `pk` order to avoid deadlocks. This relies on PostgreSQL's default READ COMMITTED isolation; changing the isolation level means revisiting the services.
  3. Quantities must be `Decimal` or `int` (not float, bool, str, NaN or Infinity), have at most 3 decimal places, and have an absolute value below 1,000,000,000. They are stored with 3 decimals. A packet variant (`is_sold_loose` false) takes whole numbers only. A loose variant takes any 3-decimal amount; its `quantity_step` applies to carts, not to the ledger. `reason` is stripped of surrounding whitespace and limited to 200 characters; `batch_number` is limited to 60.
  4. Functions: `get_on_hand(variant)`; `receive_stock` (positive quantity; optional supplier, batch number, expiry date and reason; an archived supplier is rejected; the expiry date must be a `date`, and a past date is allowed); `adjust_stock` (signed quantity, reason required); `record_damage` (positive input stored as a negative movement, reason required, may not take on-hand below 0); `count_stock` (compares a counted quantity with on-hand under the lock and writes one `ADJUSTMENT` for the difference, nothing if equal, reason required).
  5. Negative stock: only `adjust_stock` may leave on-hand below 0, because it carries an explicit reason, and it logs a warning. `count_stock` never can (a counted quantity must be 0 or more), and neither can `record_damage` or later sales. `record_damage` checks "on-hand already negative" before "more than on-hand".
  6. Archived variants can still be received and adjusted, so discontinued stock can be written off.
  7. Errors: `InventoryError` (subclass of `ValueError`), with `InvalidQuantityError` and `InsufficientStockError` below it.
  8. `find_integrity_problems()` (in `apps/inventory/integrity.py`, so `services.py` stays the only module that writes movements) and the management command `check_inventory_integrity` (it raises `CommandError`, which gives exit code 1) are read-only and never fix anything. ERROR: a fractional movement on a non-loose variant; an `ADJUSTMENT` whose reason is only whitespace; a supplier on a movement that is not a purchase. WARNING: a variant with negative on-hand. The command exits with code 1 only when there is an ERROR.
  9. Not built in Slice 16: writers for `SALE` (Phase 3/4, exactly one function), `IN_STORE_SALE` and `RETURN`. They will reuse `_record_movement`.
  10. The ledger already records who, when and why for every stock change (`created_by`, `created_at`, `reason`), so the AuditLog planned for Slice 17 should not duplicate stock movements.
- **Why:** A single write path under one lock is the only way the "check, then write" step stays safe when two people act on the same variant at once; a lock only protects code that takes it, so nothing may bypass the services. Rejecting floats and extra decimals keeps stored quantities exact, and the packet-versus-loose rule keeps whole packets whole. Negative stock is allowed only through an explicit, reasoned correction, as the architecture requires, so a sale or damage entry can never hide a miscount. The integrity check finds rows that got in around the services (raw SQL, or a variant switched from loose to packet later), since the database constraints cannot express those rules. Deferred writers wait for the phases that need them.

## D44. AuditLog: what is audited, one write function, callers write the entry

- **Decision:**
  1. Stock movements are NOT audited in AuditLog; the InventoryMovement ledger already records who, when and why (D43 point 10).
  2. Audited now: changes to ProductVariant fields `mrp`, `selling_price`, `cost_price`, `gst_rate_percent` and `is_active`. Order status and others are added in later phases by calling the same function.
  3. `core.AuditLog` fields: `created_at`, `actor` (nullable FK to the user model, `on_delete=SET_NULL`), `action` (short text), `target_type` ("app_label.model" text), `target_id` (text), `changes` (JSON, `{field: [old, new]}`), `note`. Append-only, using the same guard idea as the ledger (D42). Only `apps/core/audit.py` writes rows, through `record_change()`, which writes nothing when no value changed.
  4. Callers write the entry explicitly (admin `save_model` and `save_formset`, CSV import in Slice 20, bulk price update in Slice 21). No signals and no `save()` overrides, because queryset `update()` skips both and business logic lives in services.
  5. AuditLog is append-only in Django only; raw SQL can still change rows (same limitation as the ledger).
  6. Variant edits use the action `VARIANT_UPDATE`. The audited field list and the variant helpers live in `apps/catalog/audit.py`, so the CSV import and the bulk price update can reuse them. `AppendOnlyError` moved to `apps/core/models.py` so that `core` never imports from `inventory`; inventory imports it from core.
  7. Scope of Slice 17: only edits of existing variants (on their own admin page and inside the product page) are audited. Creating or deleting a variant and changes to other models write no entry. `cost_price` is audited only from the variant's own admin page, because the product-page inline does not show it.
  8. The admin shows the log read-only. Nobody can add, change or delete entries there, and reading needs the `core.view_auditlog` permission, because the entries contain cost prices.
- **Why:** The brief says "everything auditable", but duplicating stock movements is unnecessary and would make the ledger and the audit log disagree; the ledger already covers stock, so this narrows that brief by leaving stock to the ledger and order status to Phase 4. Explicit caller writes survive bulk updates (`queryset.update()`) and CSV imports, which signals and `save()` overrides both skip. One write path through `record_change()` is easy to review, and the function is intentionally silent when nothing changed so callers do not have to guard the no-op case.

## D45. Inventory admin: proxy screens, read-only history, write pages that call the services

- **Decision:**
  1. All inventory admin code lives in `apps/inventory/admin.py` (screens), `apps/inventory/forms.py` (forms) and, if a view grows, `apps/inventory/admin_views.py`. `ProductVariantAdmin` in `apps/catalog/admin.py` is not changed, so variant edits keep their audit rows (D44) and a stock change never goes through the variant's edit page.
  2. `InventoryMovementAdmin` is read-only: no add, change or delete, and no `has_view_permission` override (reading needs `inventory.view_inventorymovement`; superusers have it). It lists newest first, can be filtered by movement type, supplier and date, and can be searched by SKU, product name, reason and batch number.
  3. `SupplierAdmin` is an ordinary admin (name, phone, notes, `is_active`). Suppliers are archived with `is_active`, never deleted (a supplier with movements cannot be deleted anyway, because the foreign key is `PROTECT`).
  4. The "Stock" screen is a proxy model `inventory.StockItem` of `ProductVariant`, registered as its own admin. It is read-only (no add, change or delete) and shows SKU, product, unit, on-hand, low-stock threshold and a low-stock marker. On-hand there is one annotated query (`Coalesce(Sum("movements__quantity_change"), 0)`), not one query per row, and a test proves that it equals `get_on_hand`. A variant is low stock when a threshold is set and on-hand is less than or equal to it (D42, point 6). A proxy model gets its own permissions, so a packer can later be allowed to see stock without being allowed to edit prices (Phase 5 roles).
  5. Stock changes are separate admin pages under the Stock screen: receive (`receive_stock`), adjust (`adjust_stock`), record damage (`record_damage`) and count (`count_stock`). Each is a plain Django form that calls the service with `created_by=request.user`. The admin never inserts movements itself (D43, point 1). A service error (`InventoryError` and its subclasses) is shown as a form error and nothing is written. Each page needs `inventory.add_inventorymovement` and returns 403 without it. Every page is wrapped with `self.admin_site.admin_view(...)`, so login and CSRF checks apply.
  6. The expiry screen is a second proxy model `inventory.ExpiringLot` of `InventoryMovement`, read-only. It lists received lots that have an expiry date, whose variant still has stock on hand, ordered by expiry date, with filters "already expired", "within 7 days", "within 30 days" and "within 60 days". It cannot say how much of a lot is left (D42, point 4), so it shows the variant's total on-hand beside the lot.
  7. Nothing is sent when stock is low or near expiry. The alerts are screens only; notifications come with Phase 6.
- **Why:** Screens that only read are safe to hand to any staff member, and keeping every write behind the services keeps the lock and the single write path from D43 intact. A proxy model is the standard Django way to get a second admin screen, with its own permissions, over the same table without a new table. Separate pages are needed because each stock action needs different inputs for one variant (batch, expiry, reason, a counted number), which an admin list action over many rows cannot ask for. Keeping `ProductVariantAdmin` untouched avoids mixing price edits (audited) with stock actions (ledger).
- **Added while building (Slice 18):**
  - The Stock screen has an Actions column: Receive, Adjust, Count, Damage and History. Each link is shown only to a user who may open the page behind it. The four write pages need both the Stock view permission and `inventory.add_inventorymovement`; the History link needs `inventory.view_inventorymovement` and opens the movement history filtered to one variant (`?variant__id__exact=<id>`). The write pages also show a "View movement history" link under the same rule.
  - The four write pages share one view (`_stock_form_view`) that checks permissions, loads the variant (404 if unknown), calls the service through a small `apply` function and redirects to the Stock screen with a success message. A service error is shown on the form and nothing is written.
  - Count asks for the total counted on the shelf (zero or more) and a reason; the service writes an adjustment for the difference, or nothing if the count already matches. Receive accepts an expiry date in the past (open question default). Adjust can take stock below zero, as `adjust_stock` allows (D43); Damage cannot.
  - Expiring lots: "already expired" means before today; "within N days" includes today and day N. Dates use the shop's time zone (`Asia/Kolkata`). The variant's total on-hand comes from one subquery, not one query per row.
  - Migration `0003_stock_proxy_models` creates the two proxy models. They have no tables of their own.

## D46. CSV export: one row per variant, UTF-8 with BOM, formula guard

- **Decision:**
  1. The export service is `apps/catalog/csv_export.py`. It does no HTTP work: `write_variants_csv(variants, file)` writes into any object with a `write` method, so the admin passes a response and tests pass a `StringIO`. `variants_for_export` sorts by SKU and loads product, brand and category in one query (a test proves the query count does not grow with the number of rows).
  2. One CSV row is one variant. `CSV_COLUMNS` is the only column list; the Slice 20 import reads the same names, so an exported file can be edited and imported back. Product, brand and category details sit on the same row (slug as the key, name for reading). Both `is_active` flags are exported (`product_is_active`, `variant_is_active`), so archived rows are visible.
  3. The file is UTF-8 with a byte order mark, so Excel on Windows shows Devanagari correctly. Readers must open it with `encoding="utf-8-sig"` (the Slice 20 import included).
  4. Numbers are plain digits with the stored decimal places (`95.00`, `1.000`), never scientific notation; a missing value is an empty cell; flags are `TRUE` and `FALSE`.
  5. **CSV injection guard (REVIEW CAREFULLY):** a text cell that starts with `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab or a carriage return gets a single quote in front, so Excel and Google Sheets show text instead of running a formula. Number and flag columns are never touched. The Slice 20 import must undo this: remove one leading `'` from a text cell when the next character is one of those six. Accepted edge case: a real name that already starts with `'` followed by one of them would lose the quote on import (very unlikely).
  6. Two admin actions, both using the same service: "Export the selected variants to CSV" on the variant list, and "Export all variants of the selected products to CSV" on the product list. They are offered only to users who have `catalog.view_productvariant` (the file shows cost prices, and the variant pages already do). Superusers pass. Django does not run an action the user may not use. The response is `text/csv`, an attachment named `variants-<date>.csv` (shop's date), and sends `Cache-Control: no-store`.
  7. The export has no stock column. Stock is derived from the ledger (D42) and belongs to a separate stock export; the catalog app does not import inventory code.
- **Why:** One row per variant is what a spreadsheet user expects and what the billing-software import will need. A single column list, kept in code, makes the export and the later import agree by construction. The byte order mark and the formula guard are the two known ways a harmless-looking CSV goes wrong for a shop that edits files in Excel: unreadable Hindi, and a pasted `=...` running on the owner's PC.
- **Known limits:** Excel changes some values when a CSV is opened by double-click: a 13-digit barcode turns into `8.9E+12` and an HSN code loses its leading zero (`0902` becomes `902`). The import (Slice 20) must validate barcodes (digits only) and HSN codes (4 to 8 digits) and report bad rows instead of guessing. Staff should open exports in Google Sheets or LibreOffice, or import the file in Excel with those columns set to Text. An `.xlsx` export would avoid this; it needs a new dependency and is an open question.

## D47. CSV import: dry run, then all-or-nothing commit, audited

- **Decision:**
  1. The service is `apps/catalog/csv_import.py` (no HTTP work, like the export) with three steps: parse (bytes to rows), plan (check every row against the database, write nothing) and apply (write). The admin gets an "Import CSV" page on the variant list: upload, read the report, then press Confirm.
  2. File rules: UTF-8 (a BOM is accepted), at most 2 MB and 5,000 data rows. The header must contain exactly the columns of `CSV_COLUMNS`, in any order; a missing, unknown or repeated column is an error for the whole file. The delimiter may be a comma, semicolon or tab, taken from the header line (Excel on a German-locale PC saves semicolons). Decimal commas, thousands separators and scientific notation are rejected with a clear message and never guessed. Empty lines are skipped.
  3. Matching: a variant is matched by `sku_code` (exact); a known SKU is updated, an unknown one is created. SKUs missing from the file are never touched (nothing is archived or deleted by omission; use `variant_is_active` = `FALSE`). A product is matched by `product_slug` and created if missing; rows of one product must agree on the product columns. Brands and categories are matched by slug and must already exist; the import never creates them, and `brand_name` and `category_name` are ignored. A SKU that points at a different product than the stored one is an error. A SKU that differs from another only by letter case is an error.
  4. Cell rules: surrounding spaces are stripped; the export's formula guard is undone (remove one leading `'` when the next character is `=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, tab or carriage return, D46 point 5); flags are `TRUE` or `FALSE` in any case; barcodes are digits only (the message names the Excel `8.9E+12` case); HSN codes are 4 to 8 digits and not 5 or 7 digits, because Excel drops a leading zero (`09021010` becomes `9021010`) and the shorter code would otherwise pass (CA to confirm); numbers must fit the model's digits and decimal places; control characters are refused; a SKU or barcode twice in the file, or a barcode used by another variant, is an error. The model validators run (`full_clean()`) and the database constraints stay as the backstop.
  5. Dry run and commit (REVIEW CAREFULLY): the first step writes nothing and shows, for each row, CREATE, UPDATE (with the changed fields), UNCHANGED or ERROR (line, column, message), plus totals. Confirm re-reads the same file (kept server-side in the session, checked by its SHA-256), plans again inside `transaction.atomic()`, and refuses when the new plan differs from the one the user saw. The variants to update are locked with `select_for_update()` in ascending `pk` order (D43). One error anywhere means nothing is written.
  6. Audit: an update calls `snapshot_variant_from_db` before and `record_variant_change` after (D44), with the note `CSV import: <file name>` cut to 200 characters. A created variant gets one new `VARIANT_CREATE` row (old values `None`, helper in `apps/catalog/audit.py`). This narrows D44 point 7: variants created by the import are audited, variants created on the admin pages still are not. Products, brands and categories are not audited.
  7. Permissions: the page needs add and change on both product and variant (superusers pass). The view checks them itself and is wrapped with `admin_site.admin_view`, so login and CSRF apply.
  8. The file has no stock column, so the import never writes the ledger. Opening stock goes in through `receive_stock` or `count_stock` (D43).
- **Why:** The import changes prices and tax data in bulk, so it is built to be reviewed first and to fail as a whole: a half-applied file is worse than none. Matching on exact keys and refusing unknown brands and categories stops a typo from creating junk rows. Re-planning under the lock covers the time between preview and commit. The Excel and German-locale problems (semicolons, decimal commas, barcodes in scientific notation, lost zeros) are known ways a harmless-looking file goes wrong, so each gets a clear error instead of a guess.
- **Known limits:** Every column must be present, so a price-only file is not accepted yet (Slice 21 has the bulk price action). The HSN odd-length rule is a default awaiting the CA. Archived rows are only reached through the flags in the file. Nothing checks that a file came from the billing software; a mapping step for its format waits for a sample item export.
- **Added in 20c-20f:** a barcode that another SKU already holds is refused ("clear it there first"), which also covers two SKUs of one file swapping barcodes; products are locked before variants, each in ascending `pk` order, and Confirm re-plans with those locks held and refuses when the fingerprint differs from the preview; archived brands and categories are still accepted (only the slug must exist); product, brand and category changes are not audited, only the variants the import creates or changes.

## D48. Bulk price update: preview, then an all-or-nothing write, audited

- **Decision:**
  1. The service is `apps/catalog/price_update.py` (no HTTP work, like the export and the import). `plan_price_update(variant_pks, spec)` reads the selected variants with one query and returns a `PricePlan` (rows, skipped rows, problems, warnings) without writing; `apply_price_update(variant_pks, spec, *, actor, expected_fingerprint=None)` writes and returns how many variants changed. The admin action "Change prices of selected variants" lives on the variant list only and needs `catalog.change_productvariant`.
  2. A run changes **one** field: `selling_price`, `mrp` or `cost_price`. Three methods: a percentage change (`old * (1 + value/100)`), an amount change in rupees (`old + value`, plus or minus) or an exact price. The value is a `Decimal` with at most 2 decimals. For percent and amount the result is rounded half-up to a step of 1, 0.50 or 0.01 (default 1); "set exact" is not rounded. The spec is checked before any row is read: more than 2 decimals, a step outside the three, and a percentage of -100 or lower are problems.
  3. Two steps in the admin, the standard intermediate page: choose the field, method, value and step, then Preview, which shows old to new per row, the skipped rows, the problems and the warnings and writes nothing. Confirm appears only when the plan is ok and has at least one row. The page has no URL of its own: both buttons post back to the variant changelist with a hidden `action=change_prices`, the selected ids as `_selected_action` and the spec fields, and `admin/catalog/productvariant/change_prices.html` renders it.
  4. All or nothing, at most 1,000 variants per run. The admin counts the queryset before it loads the ids and refuses an oversized selection with a message; the service refuses the same way for any caller.
  5. Row rules: a new `selling_price` must not exceed the stored `mrp`, and a new `mrp` must not be below the stored `selling_price` (the other field keeps its stored value); `selling_price` and `mrp` must be more than 0; `cost_price` may be 0; the value must fit 10 digits with 2 decimals (at most 99,999,999.99). A row whose `cost_price` is blank under a percent or amount change is skipped with a reason; "set exact" fills the blank in. A row whose result equals the old value is unchanged: it is not a change, not a skip and not a problem, so it is not listed.
  6. A warning (not a block) when a row moves by more than 50 percent; a zero old value counts as a large move.
  7. Apply (REVIEW CAREFULLY): `try` outside `transaction.atomic()`; lock the variants with `select_for_update()` in ascending `pk` order (D43); plan again under the lock; compare the preview fingerprint, a SHA-256 of the spec plus each row's `pk`, `updated_at`, old and new price, and refuse with `PlanChanged` when it differs. Per changed row: `snapshot_variant_from_db` before, `full_clean()`, `save(update_fields=[field, "updated_at"])`, then `record_variant_change` after. The note is `Bulk price update: <short description>` (for example `Bulk price update: selling price +10% (round to 1)`) cut to 200 characters. `queryset.update()` is never used: it would skip both the audit row and `updated_at`.
  8. No session: the Confirm post repeats the selected ids, the spec and the fingerprint. A missing or stale fingerprint fails the comparison, nothing is written and the page explains why.
- **Why:** Prices are money, and a bulk edit is the easiest way to make a thousand mistakes at once. Planning first shows every old and new value before anything is written, and the all-or-nothing transaction means one bad row (a price above MRP, a blank cost) cannot leave half a shop repriced. Rounding half-up is what a shopkeeper expects (10.50 with a step of 1 becomes 11). The three methods are the three ways a shop actually reprices: a percentage for a supplier increase, rupees for a round-off, an exact price for a shelf change. Re-planning under the lock and comparing the fingerprint cover the minutes between the preview and the click on Confirm, so an old page can never overwrite somebody else's edit. The write goes through the model and the audit helpers rather than `queryset.update()`, because a bulk change that leaves no audit row is the one change the shop cannot explain later (D44).
- **Known limits:** One field per run: changing the price and the MRP together (for a GST change, say) needs two runs, and the first can be refused while the other field still blocks it. There is no price-only CSV: the import (D47) requires every column, and this action replaces that use rather than extending the file format. Products, brands, categories and stock are not touched: no product status, no image, no ledger row, so a price change never moves stock. The 50 percent warning threshold is fixed, not configurable. There is no undo and no bulk history screen: the AuditLog rows are the history, and reverting means running the action again with the old values. Skipped rows are listed on the page but not in the change count, and nothing records them afterwards. Archived variants are included, because the variant list shows them.
- **Added while building (21a-21f):** `PriceSpec`, `PriceRow`, `Skipped`, `Problem`, `PricePlan` (with `ok` and `fingerprint()`), `DEFAULT_STEP`, `STEPS`, `MAX_VARIANTS`, `PriceUpdateRejected` and `PlanChanged` are the module's public names. A run over the whole selection posts one ticked row plus `select_across=1`, because Django's `DATA_UPLOAD_MAX_NUMBER_FIELDS` (1,000) answers 400 when every id is posted as its own field; that is also why the admin counts before loading ids. The fingerprint covers `updated_at`, so an edit to a field this run does not change (a GST rate, for example) still invalidates the preview. Tests cover the maths, the row rules, the audit row, the lock (a thread test), the stale fingerprint and the admin page end to end.

## D53. Product search

- **Decision:** (Slice 26) Product search behind a small interface, starting with PostgreSQL full-text and trigram (D26).
  - Interface: `apps/catalog/search.py` has `SearchHit` (frozen: product_id, score), a `SearchBackend` Protocol with `search(query, limit)` (best first) and `PostgresSearchBackend`. `get_search_backend()` reads the dotted path in `SEARCH_BACKEND` (getattr default `"apps.catalog.search.PostgresSearchBackend"`), so an embedding backend can replace it without touching the storefront. The backend returns candidate ids only (at most `SEARCH_CANDIDATE_LIMIT = 200`); it knows nothing about visibility, stock or prices.
  - Query: `normalise_query` strips, collapses whitespace, lower-cases, cuts to 100 characters and keeps six words; under two characters counts as empty and the page shows a hint with no product query. Words are split on whitespace only; a `\w` regex would break Devanagari because Python's `\w` does not match vowel signs. `build_prefix_tsquery` removes tsquery syntax characters, drops empty words and makes the last word a prefix (`w1 & w2:*`); when nothing is left it returns no hits because an empty `SearchQuery` raises.
  - Three match rules, OR-ed: (a) full-text on the `simple` configuration over name_en, name_hi, brand name, category name and search_keywords, every word required and the last one a prefix; (b) `TrigramWordSimilarity(query, field) >= SIMILARITY_THRESHOLD` (0.4) on name_en, brand name or search_keywords; (c) `icontains` of the whole normalised query on name_en, name_hi or search_keywords. The `simple` configuration does no stemming and no stop words; Django's `__search` and `__trigram_similar` lookups are not used because `django.contrib.postgres` is not in INSTALLED_APPS.
  - Score is one float with named weight constants: exact name (case-insensitive) beats a name prefix beats any other full-text match beats an icontains-only or trigram-only match; ties break on name_en then pk. PostgreSQL returns a tiny rank (about 1e-20) for a non-match, so full text compares against `MIN_SEARCH_RANK` instead of zero.
  - Visibility: the storefront intersects the ranked ids with `visible_products` (D50), adds `product_ids` as a keyword argument, and sorts by relevance in the database with a `Case/When` over the ids (capped at 200). Search results use the normal listing filters (D51), pagination and 24 per page; the relevance sort is offered only on the search page, and an unknown sort still falls back to name.
  - `/search/?q=` (name `product_search`) shows a title `Search: "<q>" — Badshah Store`, the heading `Search results for "<q>"`, the count, `<meta name="robots" content="noindex">`, and a no-results block with "Check the spelling or try a shorter word" plus links to /products/ and the active top-level categories. The header has a GET search form on every page, pre-filled on the search page. htmx requests get only the results block with `Vary: HX-Request`.
  - Migration `0009_enable_pg_trgm` adds the `pg_trgm` extension only; no indexes yet (the catalogue is small, a GIN index is a documented later step).
  - No search words are logged and `/search/` has no rate limit in this slice.
  - Amended in Slice 28: rule (b) now applies only when the longest query word has at least 5 characters (`TRIGRAM_MIN_WORD_LENGTH` in `apps/catalog/search.py`), because a 3-letter word has only four trigrams and two of them are the padded start, so "tea" scored 0.5 against "tel" and "test".
- **Why:** Typos, Hinglish spellings and prefixes work without new infrastructure, and the interface keeps the door open for embeddings (D26).

## D56. Marking variants loose in bulk

- **Decision:** (Slice 29c) Two admin actions on the variant list mark the selected variants as sold loose or as packets, in bulk, with no intermediate page.
  - Service: `set_variants_loose(pks, *, loose, actor)` in `apps/catalog/loose_marking.py` returns a frozen `LooseMarkResult` (`changed`, `unchanged`, and `skipped`, a list of `LooseSkip` with `sku_code` and `reason`). It refuses more than `MAX_VARIANTS` (reusing the price update's limit) with its own `LooseMarkingRejected`, and runs inside one `transaction.atomic()`.
  - It locks the selected rows with `select_for_update()` in ascending `pk` order, decides each row under the lock, and writes only the rows that change, using `save(update_fields=["is_sold_loose", "quantity_step", "updated_at"])`. `queryset.update()` is never used (D43, D44).
  - A variant newly marked loose gets `quantity_step = 0.001`, one gram, as the D55 amendment fixed. A variant that is already loose is left unchanged, including its own step, and writes no audit row. A row whose unit is not `KG` or `L`, or whose pack size is not 1, is skipped with a reason and never written, because the database constraint `catalog_variant_loose_unit_and_pack_size` would reject it.
  - Marking as a packet sets `is_sold_loose=False` and `quantity_step=1`, which the database requires; an already-packet variant is left unchanged.
  - Auditing: `is_sold_loose` and `quantity_step` are appended to `AUDITED_VARIANT_FIELDS`, extending the audited list of D44 point 2 from five fields to seven. Every change takes `snapshot_variant_from_db` before and `record_variant_change` after, with the note "Marked sold loose (admin action)" or "Marked as packet (admin action)".
  - Admin: the two actions carry `permissions=["change"]` and repeat the `has_change_permission` check inside the action, because a post can be forged. Each counts the queryset before loading ids and refuses more than `MAX_VARIANTS`, then shows a success message with the changed and unchanged counts. When rows were skipped it adds one warning listing at most 10 SKUs with their reasons and "and N more" for the rest.
  - Narrows D55: D55 called this Slice 29b and it is numbered 29c; no rule changed.
- **Why:** Keeping the loose set as admin data means the owner maintains it without a code change, and a bulk action matches how the real list arrives (many variants at once, then edits over time). Skipping an ineligible row instead of refusing the whole run lets one action fix the rest of the selection and still say which rows it left alone. Recording both new fields in the audit log keeps a loose/packet switch as traceable as a price change (D44), because it changes what a customer may buy and in what steps. Updating the step in the same write stops a variant from being loose with a packet-sized step.
- **Known limits:** Unmarking a loose variant while carts or reservations hold quantities of it is handled when carts are built (Slice 31); nothing on the website sells a variant yet, so no such row can exist today. The exact loose list is still the owner's data (open question). A run writes its rows one at a time rather than in bulk, which is fine under the `MAX_VARIANTS` cap but is not designed for the whole catalogue.

## D57. Address: a customer's saved delivery addresses

- **Decision:** (Slice 29d, built in Slice 29f) An `Address` model in `apps/accounts` holds the delivery addresses a customer saves and reuses at checkout.
  - Fields: `user` (FK to `accounts.User`, `on_delete=models.CASCADE`, `related_name="addresses"`), `recipient_name`, `recipient_phone`, `line1` (house, flat or building), `line2` (street or area, optional), `landmark` (optional), `city` (default "Indore"), `state` (default "Madhya Pradesh"), `pincode` (6 digits, first digit not 0), `label` (choices `HOME`, `WORK`, `OTHER`) and `is_default`.
  - It inherits `TimeStampedModel` and `ArchivableModel` from `apps/core/models.py`, so it has `created_at`, `updated_at` and `is_active`. The customer deletes an address by archiving it (`is_active=False`); the row is never removed.
  - `recipient_phone` is stored the way every other phone in the project is: the form calls `normalise_phone` (`apps/core/phone.py`) and saves the `+91XXXXXXXXXX` result, and it starts pre-filled with the user's own phone because most orders go to the account holder.
  - The text fields accept Devanagari and any other script. There is no ASCII-only validator anywhere on this model, because a name and an address are written by the customer in the script they use.
  - At most one default address per user, counting only active addresses: a partial unique constraint on `user` with the condition `is_default=True AND is_active=True`. The database, not application code, is what stops two defaults from existing.
  - A customer keeps at most 5 active addresses. The limit is `MAX_ACTIVE_ADDRESSES = 5` in the service, not a column and not a database constraint, because it is a policy number the owner may change (open question, ask again before Slice 29f).
  - All reads and writes go through a service in `apps/accounts/services.py` that takes the user as its first argument: list the user's active addresses, create one, update one, archive one, set the default. A by-id call such as `get_address(user, pk)` filters on both `user` and `pk` and returns "not found" for an address that belongs to someone else. A view or form never loads an `Address` by `pk` on its own; this is the object-level check the project uses in place of a permission class.
  - The first active address becomes the user's default. Archiving the default promotes the oldest remaining active address, and switching the default clears the old one and sets the new one in one `transaction.atomic()` block, so the partial unique constraint is never transiently violated.
  - Serviceability is a service question, never a model constraint. A customer may save an address with any pin code. A service compares the pin code with the `DeliveryZone` rows (Slice 33) and answers whether it is served; the address list shows a notice beside an unserved pin, and checkout refuses that address. Saving is not refused, because a customer may move, or the shop may start serving that pin later.
  - `Order` will store a frozen copy of the address fields it used (recipient name, phone, lines, landmark, city, state, pin code, label) in Slice 35. Editing or archiving an address therefore never changes a placed order.
  - No GPS, map picker or geocoding in the MVP: the customer types the address, and a landmark is optional free text.
- **Why:** A saved address book is what makes a repeat order quick, and customers in Indore often order for a family member at another address, so the recipient name and phone are separate from the login phone. One default per user is a data rule that is safe to put in the database: it is cheap to enforce there and expensive to repair after two defaults exist. The five-address cap is a policy, so it stays a constant in one service where the owner's answer can change it without a migration. Sending every address access through a service that filters on the user prevents one customer from reading or editing another customer's address by guessing an id. Keeping the pin-code check out of the model lets the shop add or drop a pin code (Slice 33) without a migration, and lets a customer keep an old address that is no longer served. A copied address on the order is required for the GST invoice and for delivery after the customer edits or deletes the address.
- **Known limits:** `DeliveryZone` does not exist yet, so until Slice 33 every pin code is treated as served by the notice logic. Nothing checks that the address is real, and there is no map or geocoding. Duplicate addresses are not merged or refused; the 5-address cap is the only limit. If `DeliveryZone` later gains optional per-zone overrides, address saving must stay zone-blind and only the serviceability service may read zones. The exact wording of the "not served" notice and the checkout refusal is decided with the checkout UI (Slice 36).

## D58. ShopSettings: one editable row of shop-wide settings

- **Decision:** (Slice 29d, built in Slice 29e) `ShopSettings` in `apps/core` holds the shop-wide values that staff edit without a deploy. There is exactly one row.
  - Singleton: `pk` is fixed to 1 by a `CheckConstraint` named `core_shopsettings_singleton` (`pk=1`), and a `load()` accessor returns the row, creating it with its defaults on first use (`get_or_create(pk=1)`). The admin does not allow adding a second row or deleting the row (`has_add_permission` and `has_delete_permission` return False); its change page is the only write screen.
  - Identity and compliance fields, all blank allowed and blank by default because nothing may be invented in a public repository: `shop_name`, `address`, `phone`, `email`, `gstin`, `fssai_number`, `fssai_expiry` (date, null allowed), `grievance_officer_name`, `grievance_officer_phone`, `grievance_officer_email`. The owner fills them from `docs/open_questions.md`; the real GSTIN, FSSAI number, expiry and grievance details never appear in a committed file.
  - Money fields are `DecimalField` (never float): `minimum_order_value` default 200.00, `delivery_fee` default 30.00, `free_delivery_threshold` default 500.00, `cod_cap` default 2000.00. Each has a named `CheckConstraint` requiring a value of zero or more.
  - Counts and time: `reservation_minutes` default 15 (D6's configurable expiry), `online_stock_buffer` default 0, both zero or more, and `reservation_minutes` must be positive because a zero would expire a checkout immediately. `same_day_cutoff` is a `TimeField` defaulting to 17:00, compared in the project time zone (`settings.TIME_ZONE`), never in UTC and never as a literal in view code.
  - Numbering and publication: `order_number_prefix` and `invoice_number_prefix` are blank with no default, because the number-series slice (Phase 5) and the CA decide them (D55, open question). `is_published` defaults to False, so a fresh database serves no shop until the owner turns it on.
  - Read path: `get_shop_settings()` returns the row through a short cache (`django.core.cache`, key `core.shopsettings`, 60-second timeout). `ShopSettings.save()` clears that key after the write, so a staff edit is visible on the next request. The `save()` override holds cache invalidation only, no business logic; it is the one place that can catch every write to this model, and `queryset.update()` is never used on it.
  - Global rules for now: `delivery_fee`, `free_delivery_threshold` and `minimum_order_value` apply to every served pin code. This follows the D55 amendment, which fixed the same fee, free-delivery and minimum-order rules for all three pins (452012, 452014 and 453331). `DeliveryZone` (Slice 33) holds only pin codes. It may later gain optional per-zone overrides for these three values, in which case the shop-wide row is the fallback; this ADR records that as intent, not as built behaviour.
  - Auditing (implemented in Slice 29e, in the D44 style): one action name, `SHOP_SETTINGS_UPDATE`, with `target_type` "core.shopsettings" and `target_id` "1". The audited field list is a module-level tuple `AUDITED_SHOP_SETTINGS_FIELDS` in `apps/core/audit.py`, and it contains every editable field: `shop_name`, `address`, `phone`, `email`, `gstin`, `fssai_number`, `fssai_expiry`, `grievance_officer_name`, `grievance_officer_phone`, `grievance_officer_email`, `minimum_order_value`, `delivery_fee`, `free_delivery_threshold`, `cod_cap`, `same_day_cutoff`, `reservation_minutes`, `online_stock_buffer`, `order_number_prefix`, `invoice_number_prefix` and `is_published`. The admin takes `snapshot()` before, writes with `save(update_fields=[...])` and calls `record_change()` after, so a save that changed nothing writes no row.
  - Narrows D44: D44 point 7 limited auditing to variant edits in Slice 17; this adds one model, not a general rule. Narrows D55: D55 put the cutoff, the pin codes and the fee rules in data; this names where the global values live.
- **Why:** These numbers change how much a customer pays and when a delivery is promised, so they belong in one editable row rather than in settings files or in code, and the public repository must not contain the real GSTIN or FSSAI data. A singleton row with a fixed key makes "the settings" unambiguous; `load()` means a fresh install has defaults without a data migration, and the admin's blocked add and delete keep a second row from appearing. A short cache saves a query on every page while the 60-second timeout keeps a forgotten invalidation from lasting, and clearing the key in `save()` covers every write path. Auditing these fields makes a fee or cutoff change as traceable as a price change, because the same values decide what the shop charges and promises. Keeping the three delivery rules global follows the owner's answer that all three pins share one rule set today, while leaving the door open for per-zone overrides without moving the fallback.
- **Known limits:** The cache is per process, so with several workers another worker may serve the old row for up to 60 seconds after a save. There is no settings history screen; the audit log is read in the admin. No business check ties the values together yet (for example `free_delivery_threshold` being above `minimum_order_value`), so a contradictory pair can be saved and is caught only where it is used. Per-zone overrides are intent, not built. Both number prefixes stay blank until the CA and the number-series slice decide them, and a blank prefix must be treated as "not configured" rather than as an empty prefix by the Phase 5 code. The FSSAI expiry is stored but nothing reminds the owner before it lapses.

## D59. Phone OTP login

- **Decision:** (Slice 30) Customers log in with their phone number and a one-time code. Staff keep their password login at `/admin` only.
  1. Eligibility: a correct code logs in only an active account that is neither staff nor superuser. A user with `is_staff` or `is_superuser` can never log in through the OTP flow, and an inactive user cannot either; both get the same generic failure message with no hint why.
  2. Code: six digits from `secrets.randbelow`, zero-padded. Only an HMAC-SHA256 of `"phone:purpose:code"` keyed with `settings.SECRET_KEY` is stored, and verification uses `hmac.compare_digest`. The code is never stored, never logged and never put in a `NotificationLog`.
  3. Policy constants at the top of `apps/accounts/otp.py`: a code is valid for 5 minutes; 5 wrong tries kill a challenge; a phone may request again after 30 seconds; 5 requests are allowed per phone in a rolling hour. A new request marks every earlier unused challenge for that phone as consumed. Only purpose `LOGIN` exists now.
  4. The phone is normalised with `normalise_phone` before anything else. Asking for a code for a known or an unknown phone gives the identical response, so the page cannot be used to discover customers.
  5. SMS is an interface: `SmsBackend` with `send_otp(phone, code)` in `apps/notifications/sms.py`, plus `ConsoleSmsBackend` and `get_sms_backend()`, which loads the dotted path in `SMS_BACKEND`. The console backend prints `[DEV SMS] to +91******1234: code NNNNNN` with `print` only when `settings.DEBUG` is true; when DEBUG is false it raises `ImproperlyConfigured`, so production cannot silently use it. It never uses the logging module. A real MSG91 backend comes later.
  6. Flow: `GET/POST /account/login/` shows the phone form, puts the normalised phone in the session and redirects to `/account/verify/`. A correct code creates the customer when the phone is new (role MEMBER, unusable password, no staff flags) and logs in with Django's `login()`, which changes the session key; the session phone is then removed. `/account/verify/` without a session phone redirects to login. `next` is honoured only when `url_has_allowed_host_and_scheme` accepts it. `POST /account/logout/` logs out and a GET is refused with 405. A logged-in visitor to `/account/login/` is redirected home. CSRF stays on.
  7. Templates `accounts/login.html` and `accounts/verify.html` extend `base.html`; the header shows a Login link when logged out and a small POST logout form when logged in.
- **Why:** A phone plus a short code is the login customers already understand and it needs no password to remember. Keeping staff on the password login at `/admin` keeps the admin surface separate from the customer flow. Storing only an HMAC means a database copy cannot be used to log in, and constant-time comparison avoids leaking the stored value through timing. Database-backed rate limits survive restarts and a second worker. The console backend makes development possible without MSG91 credentials, while refusing to run with DEBUG off stops it reaching production silently. Identical answers for known and unknown phones stop the login page from confirming who is a customer.
- **Known limits:** There is no per-IP limit yet; add one before a real SMS provider is connected. The cart merge on login comes with the cart slice, so a cart is not yet carried across login. Rotating `SECRET_KEY` invalidates every pending code, because the HMAC key changes.

### Amendment (Slice 31, 5 November 2026)

- **Decision:** Three changes harden D59.

An advisory lock keyed by phone closes that gap without adding a row or a table. Consuming every unused challenge makes the newest code the only usable one, which is what a customer expects, and a single `update()` keeps it one statement. The login merge makes the guest cart worth building before the cart UI exists.

## D60. Cart: stores, quantity rules, availability, totals and merge

- **Decision:** (Slice 31) The cart is built from four small pieces.
  1. Two stores behind one interface. `CartStore` (an ABC in `apps/cart/stores.py`) has `lines()` and `change(callback)`. `DbCartStore(user)` stores a `Cart` row and `CartItem` rows for a logged-in customer; `SessionCartStore(session)` stores the guest cart in `request.session` under the key `guest_cart` as `{str(variant_id): "quantity string"}`. `get_store(user, session)` returns the database store for an authenticated user and the session store otherwise. The guest cart holds only ids and quantity strings because the session is JSON-safe client data: no `Decimal` objects, no prices and no names. Every page reads the live name and price from the catalogue. Insertion order is kept, so lines show in the order they were added. Reading corrupt session data never raises: an entry whose key is not a positive integer, or whose quantity is not a positive finite `Decimal` with at most three decimals, is dropped and the good entries stay.
  2. Quantity rules in `apps/cart/rules.py`, with no database access. A quantity must be a positive multiple of the variant's own `quantity_step` (0.001 for loose variants, 1 for packets), so a packet takes whole numbers and a loose item takes one-gram steps. Only `Decimal` or `int` is accepted; `float`, `bool`, `str`, NaN and Infinity are refused. At most three decimals are allowed and the stored value has three. The per-line caps are constants: `MAX_PACKETS_PER_LINE = 99` and `MAX_LOOSE_PER_LINE = Decimal("50")`. Errors are `CartError(ValueError)` with `InvalidCartQuantityError`, `QuantityTooLargeError`, `NotPurchasableError` and `InsufficientAvailabilityError` (which carries `available`). `check_cart_quantity(variant, quantity)` applies all of this; `normalise_cart_quantity` applies everything except the cap, so the merge can clamp an over-cap guest line.
  3. Availability and visibility. `apps/inventory/availability.py` returns on-hand minus `ShopSettings.online_stock_buffer`, never below zero. `get_available_quantities(ids)` is one grouped ledger query for every id and returns every requested id (0 when it has no movements), so the query count does not grow with the list; `get_available_quantity(variant)` is the one-variant wrapper. There is no reservation in this slice: Slice 34 will subtract active reservations inside the same function, so callers do not change. `apps/catalog/purchasable.py` decides visibility with the storefront rule: the variant, its product and its brand are active, and its category and every ancestor are active. It walks the parent chain with a visited set, so a loop in the data hides the variant instead of hanging. It lives in `catalog`, not `storefront`, because `cart` must not import `storefront` (storefront will import cart).
  4. Services and totals. `add_item`, `set_item_quantity`, `remove_item` and `clear_cart` take a store first. An explicit customer action that breaks a rule is refused with an exception and leaves the cart unchanged; it never silently clamps. Setting a quantity of zero removes the line. Lowering a line that is above availability is allowed. `build_cart(store)` returns frozen `CartLine` and `CartSummary` dataclasses with live prices. A line's status is checked in this order: `unavailable`, `bad_quantity`, `out_of_stock`, `not_enough_stock`, then `ok`. Only `ok` lines add to `item_total` and `savings_total`; a problem line stays in the cart with its live line total but is left out of every payable total. `bad_quantity` covers a quantity that no longer fits the variant's type or step, for example 1.500 on a packet or a value off a changed step (the D56 known limit).
  5. Money. Line total is `round_money(selling_price * quantity)` (half-up to the paisa, the Slice 13c rule); `mrp_total` and `savings` follow the same rounding. `selling_price` is GST-inclusive; for a loose variant it is the price per kg or litre. The cart holds no GST lines. The fee is `delivery_fee` below `free_delivery_threshold`, zero from the threshold up, and zero for an empty cart or a cart with no `ok` line. `free_delivery_applies` is true when the item total is **at or above** the threshold; `minimum_order_met` is true when the item total is **at or above** `minimum_order_value`. Both compare the GST-inclusive item total before the fee. `amount_to_free_delivery` and `amount_short_of_minimum` count down and stop at zero. `checkout_ready` needs at least one `ok` line, no problem lines and the minimum met. Nothing is snapshotted: every price is read live until the order (Slice 35).
  6. Merge on login. `merge_guest_cart(user, guest_items)` adds the guest and saved quantities, then caps the sum at availability and the per-line cap, then rounds down to a multiple of the variant's step. If nothing is left, or the variant is not purchasable, the line is dropped. The result is a frozen `MergeResult` with `merged`, `adjusted` and `dropped` variant-id lists and a `needs_notice` property. It runs in one transaction with the cart row locked, never changes the input dict, and an empty guest cart creates no cart row.
  7. Locking. Every `DbCartStore.change` runs in `transaction.atomic()`, creates the cart lazily with `get_or_create`, then takes `select_for_update()` on the cart row, reads the lines, calls the service's callback while the lock is held, and writes changed lines with `save(update_fields=["quantity", "updated_at"])` plus `cart.save(update_fields=["updated_at"])`. Queryset `update()` is never used for quantities. A callback exception aborts before any write, so a refused call changes nothing. The session store rewrites the whole nested dict and sets `session.modified = True`, because Django does not notice a change made inside a nested dict.
  8. No audit rows, no admin, no views and no templates for carts in this slice. Coupons, COD and delivery zones are later slices.
- **Owner defaults in use from 5 November 2026:** (1) per-line caps of 99 packets and 50 loose units; (2) the guest cart keeps Django's default 14-day session age; (3) carts untouched for a long time are never deleted yet, and a clean-up job is needed before production; (4) free delivery starts **at** the threshold, although the owner's wording said "above"; (5) the minimum order is met **at** the minimum, also inclusive. Items (4) and (5) are REVIEW points: confirm whether "above" meant strictly greater. Items (1) to (3) are low-priority open questions.
- **Why:** One interface with two stores keeps every rule in one place, so the guest and saved carts cannot drift. The session stores only ids and strings because a session is client data that must stay JSON-safe, and it keeps prices out of the session so a price change is seen immediately and the cart cannot be tampered with. A step-based rule is the only way one code can serve packets and loose goods sold by weight, and refusing an explicit bad action (rather than clamping it) tells the customer what happened. Availability behind one function means Slice 34 can add reservations without touching cart code. The purchasable rule lives in `catalog` so the dependency runs one way and the storefront can later import the cart. A live price with no snapshot is simpler than an invalidation scheme and is correct for a shop whose prices change. The merge clamps because a login must never fail or surprise the customer with an exception; the explicit-action rule stays strict. Locking the cart row makes the read-modify-write safe when a customer has two tabs open. The status order puts the most fundamental reason first, so a hidden variant reports `unavailable` even if its quantity is also bad.
- **Known limits:** The session store has no row lock: two simultaneous requests in one session (two tabs) can lose an update, because the last write wins. The cart row lock is per cart, not per variant, so checkout and reservation must re-check availability under the variant lock (Slice 34). The per-line caps, guest-cart lifetime and stale-cart deletion are defaults, not owner decisions. The inclusive threshold and minimum comparisons are a REVIEW point against the owner's "above". The merge clamps an over-cap guest line rather than dropping it, which is a deliberate reading of the merge rule. A cart line for a variant that later changed from loose to packet shows `bad_quantity` and stays until the customer fixes it (D56's known limit). There is no audit trail for cart changes and no admin screen. Because prices are live, a cart shown before a price change can differ at checkout; the order snapshot comes in Slice 35.

## D61. Cart UI: htmx forms, fragments and the header count

- **Decision:** (Slice 32) The cart becomes usable for guests and customers, with htmx and without JavaScript.
  1. URLs and views. Four global names in `apps/cart/urls.py`, included from `badshahstore/urls.py` before the storefront include: `cart_detail` (GET `/cart/`), `cart_add` (POST `/cart/add/`), `cart_update` (POST `/cart/update/`) and `cart_remove` (POST `/cart/remove/`). The three mutations use `require_POST`, every cart view uses `never_cache`, and CSRF stays on: the `<body>` `hx-headers` token covers htmx, and each plain form carries `{% csrf_token %}`.
  2. Add. The product page renders one form per addable variant. The form posts `variant_id`, `quantity` and a validated `next`; it never posts a price. htmx swaps `_add_result.html` into the `aria-live="polite"` region under the button, shows "Added 2 packs. View cart" and updates the header count out of band, so the customer stays on the product page. Without htmx the same POST adds a success message and redirects to `next` (default `cart_detail`). A bad form, an unknown variant id and any `CartError` are handled the same way in both paths; no request reaches a 500.
  3. Update and remove. Both find the line's variant with `ProductVariant.objects.filter(pk=...)` and **no** `is_active` filter, so a customer can always change or remove a line whose variant was archived after it was added. `cart_update` calls `set_item_quantity`, where zero removes the line; `cart_remove` calls `remove_item`, and removing a missing line is a no-op. On htmx both return the whole `_cart_body.html` plus the out-of-band count, so lines, problem notes, totals and the empty state refresh together. An error returns the same body with the old quantities and an alert on top. Without htmx both redirect to the cart; an update error adds a message first.
  4. Why errors are HTTP 200. htmx 2 does not swap a 4xx or 5xx response, so a form error must be a normal 200 body containing `role="alert"` or the customer would see nothing. The no-JavaScript path uses `messages.error` and a redirect instead. This comment sits in `apps/cart/views.py` too.
  5. Header count. `apps/cart/context_processors.py` returns `{"cart_line_count": <callable>}`, not a number. Django templates call a callable context value themselves, so the count is computed only on a page that prints it: a customer costs one `CartItem.objects.filter(cart__user=...).count()` query, a guest costs `len(read_guest_items(session))` and no query. The cache key is the number of lines, not the sum of quantities, because kilograms and packs cannot be added. `#cart-count` is always rendered, even at zero, so the out-of-band swap always finds a target; it shows a small badge and an `sr-only` "items in cart" above zero.
  6. Quantity text. `apps/cart/forms.py` parses request text strictly before it becomes a `Decimal`: optional surrounding spaces, then one to six digits with at most three decimals (`^\d{1,6}(\.\d{1,3})?$`). No comma, sign, exponent or letters. The cart rules then check the number itself (step, cap, availability), so the two guards stay separate. `variant_id` must be a positive integer.
  7. Wording. `apps/cart/presentation.py` holds every cart sentence. A loose amount is shown with three decimals and its own unit ("kg" or "litre"); a packet is shown as a whole number and "pack"/"packs". `describe_cart_error` maps every `CartError` subclass, and an unknown subclass gets one safe generic sentence. The message never says why a product is unavailable (archived brand, category, product or variant are all "This item is not available right now.").
  8. Presets and stepper. A loose variant gets a number field with `step` and `min` equal to its `quantity_step`, `max` equal to `MAX_LOOSE_PER_LINE`, and preset buttons 0.25, 0.5 and 1. A preset is shown only when it is an exact multiple of the step and not above the available amount. A packet gets a minus/plus stepper and a whole-number field with `inputmode="numeric"`. The default amount is 1 when the step divides it and the shop has at least 1, otherwise one step. Alpine holds the amount (`x-data`/`x-model`); with JavaScript off the input's server-rendered `value` still posts.
  9. The cart page. `cart_detail.html` extends `base.html`, is `noindex` through `head_extra`, and wraps `_cart_body.html`. A line shows a thumbnail (or the existing placeholder), a link to the product, its pack label, unit price, a quantity form, the line total and Remove. Problem lines are muted and show one note by status: `unavailable`, `out_of_stock`, `not_enough_stock` with the available amount, or `bad_quantity`. Only `ok` lines count in the totals, which come from `build_cart` alone. The totals box shows item total, delivery fee or "Free", grand total, savings above zero, the amount still needed for free delivery and the amount short of the minimum. Guests see a login hint whose `next` is the cart path. Checkout is a disabled "Checkout (coming soon)" button with no checkout URL until Slice 36.
  10. Product detail data. `VariantDetail` gains `available`, `can_add`, `is_loose`, `unit_label`, `presets`, `default_quantity` and `max_quantity`. `get_product_detail` calls `get_available_quantities` once for all variants, so the page costs one availability query, not one per variant. `in_stock` and the JSON-LD are unchanged: they still use on-hand above zero, while `can_add` (`available >= quantity_step`) also respects the online stock buffer and the step. The add form is shown only when `can_add` is true; otherwise the existing "Out of stock" badge is shown.
- **Owner defaults in use from 5 November 2026:** (1) after Add the customer stays on the product page; (2) loose presets are 0.25, 0.5 and 1, and packets use a stepper; (3) guests may view and edit the cart, and login is asked for only at checkout; (4) the header count is the number of lines, not the sum of quantities. All four were chosen as defaults by the owner; none is an open question.
- **Why:** A server-rendered cart with tiny htmx fragments keeps every price and stock check on the server, so the browser cannot send a price and the cart rules have one home. Returning the whole cart body for update and remove is simpler than patching one line: totals, problem notes and the empty state can never disagree with the lines. The out-of-band count makes the header correct after every mutation without a page reload, and rendering `#cart-count` even at zero means the swap target is never missing. A lazy context processor keeps the count query off every page that does not print it, while a guest count stays free because the session already holds the lines. Strict text parsing refuses ambiguous input (commas, exponents, signs) before the numeric rules run, which keeps the two kinds of error distinct. Looking up update and remove without `is_active` is a deliberate exception to the storefront visibility rule: an archived line must remain removable, even though adding is refused. Keeping presets to multiples of the step stops a button from ever creating an invalid quantity.
- **Known limits:** The cart page does not live-update stock while it is open; a change made in another tab or in the shop appears on the next request or refresh. There is no quantity-change confirmation for very large baskets, and no optimistic update: every change waits for the server. htmx is detected only by the literal `HX-Request: true` header. The preset list is fixed at 0.25, 0.5 and 1; it is not data. A loose preset below one step is simply hidden, so a variant with a 0.5 step shows only the 0.5 and 1 buttons. Error wording lives in code, not in the database, so staff cannot edit it. The product page still shows the old in-stock badge rule for JSON-LD even when `can_add` is false because of the stock buffer, which is intentional but can look inconsistent to a customer comparing the badge and the JSON-LD availability.

## D63. Stock reservations

- **Decision:** (Slice 34, 6 November 2026) A reservation is a hold on a variant's stock for one checkout group. It changes availability only; the ledger is untouched until a sale is written at packing (D7).
  - Model: `StockReservation` in `apps/inventory/models.py` inherits `TimeStampedModel`. Fields: `variant` (FK to `catalog.ProductVariant`, `on_delete=PROTECT`, `related_name="stock_reservations"`), `user` (FK to the user model, `PROTECT`, `related_name="stock_reservations"`), `quantity` (`DecimalField(12, 3)`), `status` (`ACTIVE`, `CONSUMED`, `RELEASED`; default `ACTIVE`), `kind` (`CHECKOUT_PENDING_PAYMENT`, `HELD_FOR_FULFILMENT`), `checkout_group` (`UUIDField`), and nullable `expires_at`, `consumed_at` and `released_at` datetimes.
  - There is no order row yet, so the rows of one checkout are tied together by `checkout_group`, a UUID. The order in Slice 35 adopts that id. A unique constraint on `(checkout_group, variant)` already matches one line per variant in an order.
  - Named constraints: `stockreservation_quantity_gt_0`, `stockreservation_unique_variant_per_group`, `stockreservation_expiry_matches_kind` (a checkout hold has `expires_at`, a fulfilment hold has none), `stockreservation_consumed_at_matches_status` and `stockreservation_released_at_matches_status` (each timestamp is set exactly when the status says so).
  - Indexes: `stockreservation_var_stat_idx` on `(variant, status)` and `stockreservation_group_idx` on `checkout_group`. Both names are 30 characters or fewer (Django's `models.E034` limit). The named index is the only index on the group column.
  - Held rule: `held` for a variant is the sum of `ACTIVE` `HELD_FOR_FULFILMENT` rows plus `ACTIVE` `CHECKOUT_PENDING_PAYMENT` rows whose `expires_at` is strictly after `now`. A hold expiring exactly at `now` no longer counts. `get_held_quantities(ids, now=None)` answers this in one grouped query for any number of variants; `get_available_quantities(ids, now=None)` and `get_available_quantity(variant, now=None)` subtract it from on-hand minus `ShopSettings.online_stock_buffer`, never below zero (D60 amendment). The query count stays constant in the number of variants.
  - Services in `apps/inventory/reservations.py`, all errors subclasses of `InventoryError`:
    - `reserve_stock(*, user, lines, kind, checkout_group=None, now=None)` runs in one transaction. It validates the lines (not empty, no duplicate variant, each quantity through `_clean_quantity` and above zero), checks that every variant exists and passes `purchasable_variant_ids`, locks the variant rows with `select_for_update()` in ascending `pk` order, refuses a group that already has rows, reads availability inside the lock, raises `ReservationShortfallError` listing every short line (nothing is written), and creates one row per line. Checkout rows get `expires_at = now + reservation_minutes`; fulfilment rows get none. A group UUID is generated when none is given.
    - `hold_for_fulfilment(checkout_group, now=None)` turns the group's `ACTIVE` checkout rows into `HELD_FOR_FULFILMENT` rows and clears `expires_at`. An unknown group raises `ReservationNotFoundError`; a group that is already entirely held returns 0 (idempotent); expired rows raise `ReservationExpiredError`; released, consumed or mixed rows raise `ReservationStateError`.
    - `release_group(checkout_group, now=None)` sets every `ACTIVE` row to `RELEASED` with `released_at`; consumed and released rows are left alone. It is idempotent, an unknown group returns 0, and it returns the number released.
    - `consume_reservation(reservation_id, now=None)` turns one `ACTIVE` `HELD_FOR_FULFILMENT` row into `CONSUMED` with `consumed_at`; anything else raises `ReservationStateError`. It writes no movement: the single `SALE` writer arrives with packing in Phase 5 and will call it in the same transaction (D7).
    - `expire_stale_reservations(now=None)` sets `ACTIVE` checkout rows whose `expires_at <= now` to `RELEASED`; it is idempotent and returns the number released. Nothing schedules it until online payments exist.
  - Every state change locks its rows with `select_for_update()`, saves each row with `save(update_fields=[..., "updated_at"])` and never uses queryset `update()`. The transitions are:

    | From | Action | To |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `ACTIVE` `CHECKOUT_PENDING_PAYMENT` | `hold_for_fulfilment` | `ACTIVE` `HELD_FOR_FULFILMENT`, no expiry |
    | `ACTIVE` `CHECKOUT_PENDING_PAYMENT` | `expire_stale_reservations` (expired) | `RELEASED` |
    | any `ACTIVE` row | `release_group` | `RELEASED` |
    | `ACTIVE` `HELD_FOR_FULFILMENT` | `consume_reservation` | `CONSUMED` |

  - The ascending `pk` lock order matters: two checkouts with the same items in a different order would otherwise take the same two locks in opposite orders and deadlock. `reserve_stock` sorts the ids before calling `_lock_variant`.
  - Stock-reducing movements (damage, adjustment, count) are not blocked by holds; physical stock is the truth. A variant whose holds exceed on-hand is reported by `find_integrity_problems()` as an `over_reserved` ERROR naming the variant, the held amount and the on-hand, and the `check_inventory_integrity` command exits with code 1 for it.
  - Owner answers on 6 November 2026 (defaults confirmed): (1) billing handoff route B: staff enter each website order into the billing software at packing, which issues the GST invoice, and the website writes its own `SALE` movement at packing in Phase 5; (2) the billing stock export is a CSV with `sku_code` and on-hand, applied as counts with a validation report, uploaded when the owner chooses. Neither is built in this slice.
  - No audit rows, no admin screens, no views and no cart or checkout changes.
- **Why:** A separate hold table keeps availability correct while the ledger stays the one record of physical stock (D5, D42). A `checkout_group` id lets the cart and checkout work exist before the order row (Slice 35) without a nullable FK that would later need a migration. The strict `expires_at > now` boundary matches the availability rule exactly, so a hold never counts for one extra instant. Locking the variant rows gives "check availability, then write" one serialisation point; the ascending order makes that lock set deadlock-free, which the concurrency tests exercise with lines in opposite orders. Writing each row through `save(update_fields=[..., "updated_at"])` keeps the timestamp truthful and stops a future edit from bypassing the model. Consuming without a movement keeps one `SALE` writer (D7) and avoids a double deduction. Reporting over-reserved variants instead of blocking damage or counts keeps physical stock the truth, because a hold is only a promise and a broken bottle must still be writable off. Recording the two owner answers here means the Phase 4 and stock-import slices start from a fixed route.
- **Known limits:** Reservations are whole-quantity only: packing with a different weight (ordered 0.500 kg, packed 0.512 kg) and partial fulfilment wait for packing in the minimal Phase 5. The expiry function exists and is tested but nothing schedules it until online payments exist. There is no per-user cap on reservations, and no audit rows are written for reservation state changes. Holds do not block damage, adjustment or count movements; the integrity check reports the over-reserved variant instead. A group is now adopted by at most one order through the unique `Order.checkout_group` (Slice 35). Two `reserve_stock` calls with the same `checkout_group` but disjoint variants do not wait for each other (a real checkout never does this).

## D64. Order, order items and the order state machine

- **Decision:** (Slice 35, 7 November 2026) Placing a COD or credit order creates the order, holds its stock and confirms it in one transaction. All order rules live in services; no `save()` override changes status.
  - Models in `apps/orders/models.py`, all inheriting `TimeStampedModel`, every foreign key `PROTECT`:
    - `Order`: `order_number` (CharField 32, unique), `user` (`related_name="orders"`), `checkout_group` (UUID, unique), `status`, `payment_method`, the frozen address (`ship_name`, `ship_phone`, `ship_line1`, `ship_line2`, `ship_landmark`, `ship_city`, `ship_state`, `ship_pincode`, `ship_label`), the frozen money (`item_total`, `delivery_fee`, `grand_total`, `savings_total`, `amount_due_on_delivery`), `promised_delivery_date`, `delivery_is_same_day`, `substitution_preference`, `customer_note`, `cancel_reason`, `cancelled_at` and `delivered_at`. Default order is newest first (`-created_at`, `-pk`). Indexes `orders_order_user_created_idx` on `(user, created_at)` and `orders_order_status_idx` on `status`.
    - `Order` statuses: `PLACED`, `PAYMENT_PENDING`, `PAID`, `CONFIRMED`, `PACKING`, `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`, `DELIVERED`, `CANCELLED`, `PAYMENT_FAILED`, `REFUND_PENDING`, `REFUNDED`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`. `payment_method` is `COD` or `CREDIT`; Phase 4 adds online payments. `substitution_preference` is `SUBSTITUTE`, `REFUND` or `CALL_ME` (default `CALL_ME`).

It is append-only: `save()` on an existing row, `delete()`, queryset `update()`/`delete()` and `bulk_update()` all raise `AppendOnlyError`, the same guard as `AuditLog` and the ledger.
  - Named constraints: `order_money_gte_0` (every money column at least 0), `order_grand_total_matches` (`grand_total = item_total + delivery_fee`), `order_cancelled_at_matches_status` (`cancelled_at` is set exactly when the status is `CANCELLED`), `order_delivered_at_matches_status` (`delivered_at` exactly when `DELIVERED`), `orderitem_quantity_ordered_gt_0`, `orderitem_quantity_fulfilled_gte_0`, `orderitem_prices_gte_0` and `orderitem_unique_variant_per_order`.

The moves are `PLACED → PAYMENT_PENDING | CONFIRMED | CANCELLED`, `PAYMENT_PENDING → PAID | PAYMENT_FAILED | CANCELLED`, `PAYMENT_FAILED → PAYMENT_PENDING | CANCELLED`, `PAID → CONFIRMED | REFUND_PENDING`, `CONFIRMED → PACKING | CANCELLED`, `PACKING → OUT_FOR_DELIVERY | CANCELLED`, `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY → DELIVERED | CANCELLED` and `REFUND_PENDING → REFUNDED | PARTIALLY_REFUNDED`. `DELIVERED`, `CANCELLED`, `REFUNDED` and `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` have no moves. `can_transition()` is true only for an allowed pair. `COD_ONLY_BLOCKED` lists every payment and refund status, so a COD or credit order can never enter one of them even though the table holds them for Phase 4.
  - Numbering in `apps/core/numbering.py`: `financial_year_label()` returns `"{start % 100:02d}-{(start + 1) % 100:02d}"`, with the year starting on 1 April, read in `Asia/Kolkata`. A naive datetime is refused. `next_number(prefix, when=None)` locks the one `NumberSeriesCounter` row for `(prefix, label)` with `select_for_update()`, increments it and returns `f"{prefix}/{label}/{last_number:06d}"`, for example `BS/26-27/000001`. The counter is created on first use, the row is saved with `save(update_fields=...)`, and a rollback returns the number to the pool. A blank prefix is refused. Above 999999 the number simply grows to seven digits instead of truncating.
  - `core.NumberSeriesCounter`: `prefix` (CharField 20), `financial_year` (CharField 5) and `last_number` (PositiveIntegerField), with the named constraints `numberseries_unique_prefix_year` and `numberseries_last_number_gte_0`. A blank `ShopSettings.order_number_prefix` falls back to `BS`; a configured prefix is stripped before use.
  - Services in `apps/orders/services.py`, all errors subclasses of `OrderError(ValueError)`:
    - `place_order(*, user, address_id, payment_method, checkout_group, substitution_preference=CALL_ME, customer_note="", now=None)` runs in one transaction. It locks the user row first, then returns the existing order with `created=False` when the same customer already used the group, or raises `CheckoutGroupInUseError` when another customer used it. It refuses a missing, archived or foreign address (`OrderAddressNotFoundError`), an unserved pin code (`AddressNotServedError`), an empty cart (`EmptyCartError`), a cart that is not checkout-ready (`CartNotReadyError` with a reason) and a COD total above `ShopSettings.cod_cap` (`CodLimitExceededError`; exactly at the cap is allowed). It then calls `reserve_stock` with kind `HELD_FOR_FULFILMENT` and the same group, adopts the group from the returned rows, takes the number, builds the frozen snapshot from the cart summary and address, writes one `OrderItem` per line, writes the first log row (`"" → PLACED`), applies the `PLACED → CONFIRMED` move through the same private helper as a staff transition, and clears the cart last. The fixed lock order is the user row, then variants ascending by `pk` inside `reserve_stock`, then the new rows.
    - `transition_order(order_id, to_status, *, actor, note="", reason="", now=None)` locks the order by id, refuses an unknown order (`OrderNotFoundError`), an unknown, repeated or illegal move (`InvalidTransitionError`), a COD or credit order entering a payment or refund status and a staff cancellation with a blank reason (`CancelReasonRequiredError`). It saves with `update_fields=[..., "updated_at"]` and writes one log row. A cancel also calls `release_group` (idempotent) and marks every still-`PENDING` item `CANCELLED`.
    - `cancel_order_by_customer(user, order_id, *, reason="", now=None)` locks only the customer's own order, reports another customer's order as not found, allows only `PLACED` or `CONFIRMED`, accepts a blank reason and writes the same cancel state, log row, release and item cancellation.
    - `get_user_order(user, order_id)` returns one order with `items` and `status_logs` prefetched; another customer's or a missing order raises `OrderNotFoundError`. `list_user_orders(user)` returns the customer's orders newest first.
  - A cancellation sets `cancel_reason` (stripped, cut to 200 characters) and `cancelled_at`; `DELIVERED` cannot be cancelled. A customer cancellation with a blank reason stores `""`, which the constraint allows.
  - Owner defaults in use: COD and credit; the order is `CONFIRMED` as soon as it is placed; a customer may cancel until packing starts; staff may cancel from `PLACED`, `CONFIRMED`, `PACKING` or `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`; the reason is optional for the customer and required for staff; the number series prefix is `BS` until the owner sets one; the financial year is the Indian one.
  - No packing, `SALE` movement, invoice, payment, refund, substitution execution, permission check, view, template, admin screen, URL or notification is built in this slice. `amount_due_on_delivery` is set to `grand_total` and is not recalculated yet.
- **Why:** One transaction for place, hold and confirm means a customer never sees a confirmed order with no stock behind it, and a shortfall rolls back the number, the order and the hold together. Making `checkout_group` unique puts the double-submit guard in the database as well as in the service, while the user row lock makes two identical submissions queue rather than race. Freezing the address and every price on the order matches D57 and D63: editing a product, a price or an address later cannot rewrite history. Keeping the status table in a Django-free module makes the allowed moves readable, testable and safe to import anywhere. A COD order skipping the payment states keeps Phase 3 small while leaving the Phase 4 states in the same table. Locking the counter row is the only way to keep numbers sequential without gaps; doing it inside the order transaction means a failed order uses no number. The status log is separate from the audit log because it is customer-visible history, and the append-only guard makes it as trustworthy as the ledger. Writing each row with `save(update_fields=[..., "updated_at"])` keeps timestamps truthful and stops a future edit from bypassing the model.
- **Known limits:** Placing an order is not reachable from the website yet: the checkout view, form, confirmation page and URLs arrive in Slice 36. Packing, partial fulfilment, substitutions, the `SALE` movement and finalisation wait for Phase 5; online payments and refunds wait for Phase 4. No permission check guards `transition_order`, so a caller must already be staff; views must enforce roles when they exist. `cancel_reason` is cut to 200 characters and `customer_note` to 300 without telling the caller. Order numbers are sequential within a financial year but not gapless across a database restore or manual deletion, and the six-digit padding is cosmetic: a counter of 1000000 prints seven digits. Nothing writes an `AuditLog` row: the status log is the order history. There is no customer "reorder", no order search and no notification. `get_user_order` always prefetches items and logs, even when a caller needs neither. The state table allows `PLACED → CONFIRMED` but no screen uses it directly; only `place_order` does. The state table is pinned exactly by one test; the counter lock is pinned by a query test, not only by a thread race.

Only the owner sees it; another user's group and an unknown group are a plain 404.

Refusing a foreign group exactly like a stale one avoids confirming that another customer's order exists. ## D67. Credit (khata) accounts and ledger

- **Decision:** (Slice 37a, 7 November 2026) Credit is a per-customer khata: a limit, a running balance and an append-only ledger of every change. This slice builds the models, services, integrity report and admin only; checkout and cancellation are wired to it in Slice 37b.
  1. App and scope: a new `apps/credit` app (label `credit`) with `CreditAccount`, `CreditEntry`, the services, `check_credit_integrity()` and two admin screens. No change to `apps/orders`, checkout or any customer-facing view, and no customer page.
  2. Sign convention: `CreditEntry.amount` is positive when the customer owes more (`ORDER`) and negative when they owe less (`PAYMENT`, `CANCEL_REVERSAL`). An `ADJUSTMENT` may have either sign but never zero. `credit_limit` and `balance` are `DecimalField(12, 2)` money, like every other amount in the project. A customer owes `balance` now; `available = max(credit_limit - balance, 0)`.
  3. Append-only ledger: no entry may be edited or deleted. `save()` on an existing row, `delete()`, queryset `update()`/`delete()` and `bulk_update()` raise `AppendOnlyError`, exactly like `OrderStatusLog`. A mistake is corrected by a new `ADJUSTMENT` row with a note.
  4. Cached balance: `CreditAccount.balance` is a cached column kept equal to the sum of the account's entries. Every writer locks the account row, computes `balance_after`, writes the entry, then saves the account, all in one `transaction.atomic()`. `check_credit_integrity()` proves the cache, the `balance_after` chain and each ORDER entry's amount against the order's frozen total.
  5. Charge and reverse: `charge_order(order, actor)` charges the account found through `order.user`, never an account passed by the caller. `reverse_order_charge(order, actor)` gives the credit back when an order is cancelled before delivery. Both take an optional aware `now` so tests are deterministic; the entry date is that moment's date in `Asia/Kolkata`.
  6. All or nothing: a credit order fits only when its `grand_total` is at most the available credit. Exactly equal is allowed and one paisa more raises `CreditLimitExceededError` with nothing written. The COD cap does not apply to credit.
  7. Suspended credit: a customer without an account and a customer whose account has `is_active=False` have zero available credit and cannot be charged (`CreditNotAvailableError`). Payments and adjustments are still allowed on a suspended account, because money owed is still collected. Lowering the limit below the balance is allowed; it only makes available credit zero.
  8. Payments: an amount must be positive, at most two decimals, no more than the current balance, with a mode (`CASH`, `UPI` or `BANK`), and an entry date not after today in `Asia/Kolkata`. The stored amount is negative. A reference (for example a UPI id) and a note are optional. An overpayment or refund is an `ADJUSTMENT` with a non-blank note, so the balance never goes below zero through a payment typo.
  9. No due dates, reminders or interest, and no customer page in this slice.
  10. Idempotency: the database has one partial unique constraint for ORDER rows per order and one for CANCEL_REVERSAL rows per order. Calling `charge_order` again returns the existing entry and writes nothing. Calling `reverse_order_charge` again returns the existing reversal and writes nothing, and an order that was never charged returns `None` and writes nothing. The reversal copies `-charge.amount` instead of re-reading `order.grand_total`, so a damaged order total cannot make the two rows disagree.
  11. Lock order for the whole system: the user row first, then the stock variants in ascending id, then the order rows, and the credit account row LAST. `charge_order` and `reverse_order_charge` follow it; no function locks a credit account before an order.
  12. Audit: `set_credit_limit` and `set_credit_active` write `AuditLog` rows through `record_change` with actions `CREDIT_LIMIT_CHANGED` and `CREDIT_ACTIVE_CHANGED` and the old and new values. A call that changes nothing writes no row. Creating an account counts as a limit change from the implied zero.
  13. Admin: only active staff with role OWNER or MANAGER (or a superuser) may read or write credit screens, and Django's model permissions are still required. Nothing is deletable. `balance` is read-only. Existing entries are read-only. The entry add page offers only `PAYMENT` and `ADJUSTMENT`, and its `save_model` calls `record_payment` or `record_adjustment`; a service refusal is shown as a form error, never a 500 page. Limit and status changes on the account page go through the services too.
  - Ledger rules, also enforced by named database check constraints:

    | Entry type | Sign of `amount` | Must have | Constraint |
    | --- | --- | --- | --- |
    | `ORDER` | positive | an `order` | `creditentry_order_amount_gt_0`, `creditentry_order_has_order` |
    | `PAYMENT` | negative | a non-blank `payment_mode` | `creditentry_payment_amount_lt_0`, `creditentry_payment_has_mode` |
    | `CANCEL_REVERSAL` | negative | an `order` | `creditentry_reversal_amount_lt_0`, `creditentry_order_has_order` |
    | `ADJUSTMENT` | either sign, never zero | a note (service rule) | `creditentry_amount_not_zero` |

  - Other named constraints: `creditaccount_limit_gte_0`, `creditentry_unique_order_charge` (unique `order` where the type is `ORDER`) and `creditentry_unique_order_reversal` (unique `order` where the type is `CANCEL_REVERSAL`). Foreign keys are `PROTECT`, so an account with entries and a user with an account cannot be deleted. The index on `(account, id)` is `credit_entry_account_id_idx`.
- **Why:** The shop already runs a paper or billing-software khata, and the owner asked for the website to track the same limit and balance. A ledger of signed rows makes every change explainable and lets the cached balance be proved by a check instead of trusted. Partial unique constraints make a double-click or a retried service call harmless at the database level, not only in Python. Finding the account through the order's user removes a whole class of "charged the wrong customer" bugs. Fixing the lock order now, before Slice 37b adds checkout and cancellation, avoids a deadlock that would be hard to reproduce. Refusing a payment above the balance keeps the running balance readable to the shop; a genuine refund is a visible adjustment with a reason.
- **Known limits:** There is no customer page, dues list, reminder or interest (D67 point 9). Checkout and cancellation do not use the services until Slice 37b, so no order is charged by the website yet. The balance is a cached column; a writer that bypasses the services would be caught only by `check_credit_integrity()`, which is run by hand. A payment cannot exceed the balance, so refunds and overpayments must be adjustments. There is one currency (INR) and one account per user, with no multi-user household accounts. A migration (`0001_initial`) is pending: the owner generates and runs it.

## D68. Credit at checkout and cancel reversal

- **Decision:** (Slice 37b, 7 November 2026) A customer with an active khata can choose credit at checkout, the order is charged in the same transaction, and cancelling the order reverses the charge. The credit integrity report also checks credit orders against their ledger rows.
  1. `Order.PaymentMethod` gains `CREDIT = "CREDIT", "Credit (khata)"`. The status flow is unchanged: a credit order is created `PLACED` and confirmed in the same transaction, exactly like COD.
  2. `place_order` accepts only `COD` or `CREDIT`. Any other value raises `InvalidPaymentMethodError` (a subclass of `OrderError`) after the same-group lookup and before any address, cart or stock read or write.
  3. A credit order ignores `ShopSettings.cod_cap`. Its `grand_total` must fit `available_credit` (limit minus balance, never below zero), all or nothing; exactly equal is allowed and one paisa more is refused. Its `amount_due_on_delivery` is `0.00`. A COD order keeps the cap and stores `grand_total` as the amount due.
  4. `place_order` calls `apps.credit.services.charge_order(order, actor=user)` after the order and its items exist and before the first `OrderStatusLog` row. `CreditNotAvailableError` becomes `OrderCreditUnavailableError` and `CreditLimitExceededError` becomes `OrderCreditLimitExceededError`; both are `OrderError` subclasses, so views never import a credit app error. Because the whole call is one transaction, a refused charge rolls back the order, items, reservation, order number and cart clearing: no order, no reservation, no entry and the next order number is unchanged.
  5. The cancel hook: in `_apply_transition`, for a move to `CANCELLED`, right after `release_group(...)` and `_cancel_pending_items(order)`, the service calls `apps.credit.services.reverse_order_charge(order, actor=actor, now=now)`. It is idempotent and writes nothing for an order that was never charged, so every COD cancel is unchanged. It covers staff cancels from `PLACED`, `CONFIRMED`, `PACKING` and `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` and customer cancels. A `DELIVERED` order cannot be cancelled, so its charge stays.
  6. `COD_ONLY_BLOCKED` applies when the payment method is `COD` or `CREDIT` (the constant keeps its name) and the refusal reads "A COD or credit order cannot move to X".
  7. `CheckoutForm` gains `payment_method` with the model's choices, `required=False`, initial `COD`. A missing value means `COD`; any other value is a form error with `PAYMENT_METHOD_INVALID`.
  8. Checkout GET offers credit only to a customer with an active `CreditAccount`. A customer without an account, or with a suspended one, sees no payment radios at all and the page is exactly the Slice 36b page. When credit is offered, the page shows "Available credit: ₹X" and one radio per method. COD is disabled with the COD-limit note when the total is above `cod_cap`; credit is disabled with "Your available credit ₹X is below this order" when the total does not fit. The default is COD when COD is allowed, otherwise credit when it fits. The submit button appears only when at least one method can be selected. The sentence next to each radio is "Pay ₹X in cash when your order arrives." for COD and "₹X will be added to your khata balance. Nothing to pay on delivery." for credit, so no JavaScript is needed.
  9. The checkout POST steps do not change. `place_order` receives `form.cleaned_data["payment_method"]`. `OrderCreditUnavailableError` and `OrderCreditLimitExceededError` re-render the checkout page with HTTP 200, keep the customer's input and the session group, and create nothing. The limit message reads `available_credit(user)` at that moment.
  10. The confirmation page shows "Added to your khata: ₹X. Nothing to pay on delivery." and the payment name "Credit (khata)" for a credit order. A COD confirmation page is unchanged.
  11. `check_credit_integrity()` adds three codes, reusing the entries already loaded and one credit-orders query: `credit_order_without_charge` (a `CREDIT` order that is not `CANCELLED` has no ORDER entry), `cancelled_credit_order_not_reversed` (a `CANCELLED` credit order with an ORDER entry has no CANCEL_REVERSAL entry) and `charge_on_non_credit_order` (an ORDER entry whose order is not `CREDIT`). The problem's `account_id` is the entry's account for a wrong charge and the order user's account otherwise; an order whose user has no account falls back to `0`.
  12. All new wording lives in `apps/orders/presentation.py`. The credit option, the available amount, the limit notes, the error messages and the confirmation sentence are built there, never in a template.
  13. No customer khata page, no staff order screen and no payment-in-order link are built in this slice.
- **Why:** Charging inside `place_order`'s transaction means a shop never sees a confirmed credit order that was not charged, or a charge without an order. Translating the credit errors to order errors keeps the view depending on one app and lets the page show a useful message instead of a credit-internals trace. Reversing in one place, the cancel transition, covers staff and customer cancels and every status that can be cancelled, and the idempotent service makes a retried call harmless. Offering credit only to an active account, and disabling a method that cannot be used rather than hiding it, makes the choice clear without a second page or JavaScript.
- **Known limits:** There is no customer khata page and no staff order screen, so a customer cannot see the balance and staff cannot see an order's charge from the website (the credit admin shows the ledger). A credit order's total is fixed at checkout; a change at packing needs an adjustment, which belongs to the packing slice. Credit and cash cannot be split on one order: the whole total goes to one method. Limits are checked only at checkout, so lowering a limit later does not revisit an existing order. There are no payment reminders. The cancel hook holds the order lock before `reverse_order_charge` takes the user lock, which is the reverse of the fixed user-then-order order (D67 point 11); it is safe today because no other path takes the same two rows in the opposite sequence, but a future caller of `reverse_order_charge` outside a cancel must keep that in mind. A migration for the new payment choice is pending: the owner generates and runs it.

## D69. Order notifications

- **Decision:** (Slice 38a, 8 November 2026) Order messages are written and sent from two small modules, and nothing is hooked into the order service yet (that is Slice 38b).
  1. Events are string constants in `apps/notifications/messages.py`: `EVENT_PLACED = "ORDER_PLACED"`, `EVENT_CANCELLED = "ORDER_CANCELLED"`, `EVENT_OUT_FOR_DELIVERY = "ORDER_OUT_FOR_DELIVERY"`, `EVENT_DELIVERED = "ORDER_DELIVERED"` and `EVENT_STAFF_NEW_ORDER = "STAFF_NEW_ORDER"`. `CUSTOMER_EVENTS` is a tuple of the first four; the staff event is email only.
  2. Channels: customer SMS to `order.user.phone`; customer email to `order.user.email`, skipped when empty; staff email to `ShopSettings.email`, skipped when blank. The staff event never sends an SMS.
  3. Two switches in settings, both off by default: `ORDER_SMS_ENABLED` and `ORDER_EMAIL_ENABLED` (environment-backed booleans in `base.py`; both `True` in `dev.py`). `DEFAULT_FROM_EMAIL` is `Badshah Stores <noreply@localhost>` unless the environment sets it.
  4. `notify_order_event(order_id, event)` loads the order with `select_related("user")`, with no lock and no write. Each of the three sends is inside its own `try/except Exception` that logs with `logger.exception` and moves on, so one broken channel cannot stop another and a message can never fail an order. The log line carries only the event, the order id and the channel name; never a phone number, an email address or the message text. A missing order or an unknown event logs a warning (event and id only) and returns.
  5. SMS goes through a new free-text method, `SmsBackend.send_message(phone, text)`. It is deliberately not abstract, so backends written for login codes keep working. `ConsoleSmsBackend.send_message` prints `[DEV SMS] to +91******1234: <text>` when `DEBUG` is true and raises `ImproperlyConfigured` otherwise, exactly like `send_otp`.
  6. The message builders (`sms_text`, `email_subject`, `email_body`) are plain Python with no Django imports, so the exact wording is tested without a database.
  7. No table records a sent message, and there is no retry, no queue and no Celery task.
- **Why:** An order must never fail because a message did, and a broken SMS provider must not stop the confirmation email, so every channel is isolated and the caller sees no exception. The exact texts live in one Django-free module because the owner will register them as DLT templates and any wording change must be visible in one place. Sending is synchronous because the shop sends a handful of messages a day and a task queue would add moving parts before MSG91 and a real SMTP provider exist. Keeping the switches off by default means production cannot send a real message until someone sets a real backend and turns the switch on.
- **Known limits:** No retry and no record of sent messages, so a failed send is visible only in the log. No per-customer opt-out and no channel preference. A customer receives messages only at the account phone, not at the address phone. English only. A synchronous send makes the request wait for the provider, so a slow provider slows the page. There is no DLT registration yet, so real SMS stays off until MSG91 and DLT are ready. A real SMTP provider and sender address are still open questions.
- **The six exact SMS wordings (for DLT registration):**
  1. Placed, cash on delivery: `Badshah Stores: Order {order_number} is placed. Pay Rs {amount} on delivery. Delivery: {date}.`
  2. Placed, credit: `Badshah Stores: Order {order_number} is placed on your credit account. Total Rs {amount}. Delivery: {date}.`
  3. Cancelled: `Badshah Stores: Order {order_number} is cancelled. Call us if you have questions.`
  4. Out for delivery, cash on delivery: `Badshah Stores: Order {order_number} is out for delivery. Please keep Rs {amount} ready.`
  5. Out for delivery, credit: `Badshah Stores: Order {order_number} is out for delivery.`
  6. Delivered: `Badshah Stores: Order {order_number} is delivered. Thank you for shopping with us.`

  `{amount}` is `amount_due_on_delivery` for cash on delivery and `grand_total` for the placed credit text. Amounts always have two decimals (`Rs 230.00`), dates read `07 Nov 2026`, the text is plain ASCII and one SMS part is at most 160 characters.

  **Slice 38b amendment** (8 November 2026): `apps/orders/services.py` now registers the notices with `transaction.on_commit(...)`. Each operation registers exactly one callback, and that callback sends its events in order; nothing is sent before the commit, so a rolled-back order sends nothing and a message never blocks or fails an order. Events per operation: `place_order` sends `ORDER_PLACED` then `STAFF_NEW_ORDER`, but only when it created a new order (the replay path returns earlier and sends nothing); a move to `CANCELLED` from staff `transition_order` or `cancel_order_by_customer` sends `ORDER_CANCELLED`; a move to `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` sends `ORDER_OUT_FOR_DELIVERY`; a move to `DELIVERED` sends `ORDER_DELIVERED`; `CONFIRMED`, `PACKING` and the internal `PLACED` to `CONFIRMED` move inside `place_order` send nothing. A staff cancellation therefore also sends the customer's cancel message, the default recorded in `docs/open_questions.md`. `notify_order_event` now also wraps the order load and `build_notice` in a `try/except Exception` that logs `channel=load` and returns, so a database error or a bad field cannot escape either.

## D70. Customer order pages

- **Decision:** (Slice 38c, 8 November 2026) A logged-in customer gets a "My orders" page at `/orders/` and a header link to it. This slice adds the list page only; order detail, the cancel page and the WhatsApp button come in Slice 38d.
  1. URL: `order_list` is `/orders/` (GET only) in a new `apps/orders/order_urls.py`, included in `badshahstore/urls.py` at `orders/` next to the `checkout/` include and before the storefront include. The path holds no order number, because order numbers contain slashes. `apps/orders/urls.py` is unchanged.
  2. Access: `login_required(login_url=LOGIN_URL)` and `never_cache`, so a guest is sent to login with `next=/orders/` and the page is never cached. `require_http_methods(["GET"])` makes a POST return 405. The page lists only `request.user`'s orders, so another customer's order can never appear.
  3. Paging: `ORDER_LIST_PAGE_SIZE = 10` in `apps/orders/services.py`. The view uses `Paginator(...).get_page(request.GET.get("page"))`, which turns a bad or too-large page number into page 1 or the last page by itself.
  4. Data: `list_user_order_rows(user)` is `list_user_orders(user).annotate(item_count=Count("items"))`, so the page runs the same number of queries for 1 order or 10 (no per-row query). Newest first is the model's own `-created_at, -pk` order. The aggregate annotation adds `GROUP BY`, and Django ignores `Meta.ordering` on such a query, so the service applies that same order explicitly.
  5. Rows: every row shows the order number, the placed date read in the shop time zone, the plain status sentence from `status_sentence`, the grand total through `money`, the payment method through `payment_method_label`, the item count through `item_count_text`, and a "View order" link. All wording lives in `apps/orders/presentation.py`, never in the template.
  6. Until Slice 38d each row links to the existing confirmation page with the order's `checkout_group`, because there is no order detail page yet.
  7. Customers see all their orders, including cancelled ones. The empty state shows a sentence and a "Start shopping" link to `product_list`.
  8. The header shows a "My orders" link (`data-my-orders-link`) to logged-in customers only, right after "Addresses"; guests do not see it.
- **Why:** The list page answers the first question a customer asks after ordering ("what did I order?"), and keeping every sentence in `presentation.py` means the wording is tested in one place. The annotated item count keeps the page at one order query however long the order history grows, and the path without an order number avoids the slashes in `BS/26-27/1`.
- **Known limits:** No search and no filter, so a long history is paged only. No order detail page and no cancel button yet (Slice 38d), so a row opens the confirmation page. The list shows no delivery date, address or item lines; those belong to the detail page.
- **Slice 38d amendment:** (Slice 38d, 8 November 2026) Each order now has a detail page and a cancel page, and the list rows link to the detail page.
  1. URLs: `order_detail` is `/orders/<uuid:group>/` and `order_cancel` is `/orders/<uuid:group>/cancel/`, both found by the order's `checkout_group` (the order number contains slashes). Both are `login_required`, `never_cache`; detail is GET only and cancel takes GET and POST. Another customer's order is a 404 for GET and POST alike.
  2. Timeline: one row per `OrderStatusLog`, oldest first, using the prefetched logs. Each row shows `timeline_label(to_status)` and `timeline_time_text(created_at)` in the shop time zone. It never prints the log note, the actor or the cancel reason; a status with no label falls back to `status_sentence`.
  3. Cancel page: GET shows the question, an optional 200-character reason and the confirm/keep links; GET never writes. POST repeats the two state checks first: `CANCELLED` redirects with `ALREADY_CANCELLED_MESSAGE`, any other non-cancellable status redirects with `CANCEL_NOT_POSSIBLE_MESSAGE`. An invalid reason re-renders with 200 and changes nothing. A valid POST calls the existing `cancel_order_by_customer`; `InvalidTransitionError` redirects with the cannot-cancel message and success redirects to the detail page with `ORDER_CANCELLED_MESSAGE`. A second POST hits the already-cancelled branch and never calls the service, so a credit reversal cannot run twice.
  4. `CUSTOMER_CANCELLABLE = (Order.Status.PLACED, Order.Status.CONFIRMED)` in `apps/orders/services.py` is the one rule: the service and both views read it, so they cannot disagree.
  5. WhatsApp: `ShopSettings.whatsapp_number` (blank by default and audited) becomes a plain `https://wa.me/<digits>?text=<message>` link, normalised through `normalise_phone` and encoded with `quote(text, safe="")`. The button is hidden when the number is blank or invalid, so no broken link is printed. There is no API and no cost.
  6. A cancelled credit order shows "₹X was returned to your khata."; a cancelled COD order shows nothing extra.
- **Known limits (38d):** No reorder button, no invoice and no staff screen (Phase 5). The customer cannot edit an order after placing it.

## D71. Account verification and the delivery gate

- **Decision:** (Slice 38e, 8 November 2026) A customer's phone number, and the email address when the account has one, must be verified before the first delivery.
  1. `User` gains two nullable timestamps, `phone_verified_at` and `email_verified_at`, with no default. `User.is_ready_for_delivery` is true when `phone_verified_at` is set and either the account has no email or `email_verified_at` is set. An OTP-only customer without an email is ready once the phone is verified.
  2. `OtpChallenge.Purpose` gains `PHONE_VERIFY`, `EMAIL_VERIFY` and `PASSWORD_RESET` (the last is used by Slice 38f). Challenges stay keyed by the account's phone whatever the delivery channel. `request_code` and `verify_code` take a keyword-only `purpose` (default `LOGIN`), and `request_code` also takes `send: Callable[[str], None] | None`; the default still sends the SMS exactly as before. The HMAC includes the purpose, and the 30-second, 5-per-hour and 5-wrong-try limits are counted per phone and purpose.
  3. Email codes come from `apps/notifications/email_codes.py`. `send_email_code` uses Django's `send_mail` with the fixed subject `Badshah Stores: your verification code` (never the code) and a plain body with the code, the five-minute validity and the "do not share" line. It always sends, independent of `ORDER_EMAIL_ENABLED`; in development the console email backend prints it. A failure is swallowed and logged with `logger.exception("Verification email failed")`, never with the code, the address or the phone.
  4. `apps/accounts/verification.py` holds the rules. `request_phone_code` and `request_email_code` issue the code, and `request_email_code` returns `sent=False, reason="no_email"` without sending when the account has no address. `confirm_phone_code` and `confirm_email_code` check the code and set the timestamp only while it is still empty, so the first verification time is kept; they never touch the password. `claim_account_by_otp_login` proves the phone on an OTP login and takes over an account whose phone was never verified: it sets `phone_verified_at`, clears the password with `set_unusable_password()` and empties `email_verified_at`. The why is that somebody may have signed up with another person's number. It logs only the user id, and returns true only when a usable password was cleared.
  5. `apps/accounts/views.py::verify_view` calls `claim_account_by_otp_login` after `_customer_for_login` and before `login()`, and shows `PASSWORD_RESET_FOR_SAFETY_MESSAGE` when a password was cleared. A brand-new OTP user is therefore verified on the spot. Staff and superusers stay refused exactly as before (the owner has not decided how staff use the shop).
  6. The delivery gate is in `apps/orders/services.py::_apply_transition`, after the allowed-move and COD/credit checks and before any write or notice: a move to `OUT_FOR_DELIVERY` when `order.user.is_ready_for_delivery` is false raises `CustomerNotVerifiedError(OrderError)`. A refused move changes nothing and sends no notice, and no other status is affected, including `CANCELLED`.
  7. Two pages, `/account/verify-phone/` (`account_verify_phone`) and `/account/verify-email/` (`account_verify_email`), share `templates/accounts/verify_contact.html` and the view in `apps/accounts/verification_views.py`. Each shows the masked contact and one of three states: verified, no email (email page only), or the two small forms ("Send me a code" and "Enter the code"). A wrong, expired or used code gives one generic message; "too soon" and "too many" share one wait message; an unknown action re-shows the page. A successful confirmation redirects back to the same page.
  8. The banner is `templates/orders/_verify_banner.html`, included on the order detail and confirmation pages only. `pending_verifications(user)` in `apps/accounts/verification_presentation.py` lists only what is missing (the phone always when unverified; the email only when the account has one and it is unverified), with a link to the matching page. The view passes `verification_items` and `verification_heading`; no extra query is added.
  9. All wording lives in `apps/accounts/verification_presentation.py`, including the masking helpers `mask_phone` (keep `+91` and the last four digits) and `mask_email` (keep the first letter and the domain), and `verification_text()` for the templates. The pages are `noindex`, `login_required`, `never_cache` and GET/POST only.
  10. In the admin, both timestamps are read-only and shown under "Important dates". `save_model` clears `phone_verified_at` when `phone` changed and `email_verified_at` when `email` changed, so a changed contact detail must be verified again; a save with no changes leaves both untouched.
- **Why:** One verified contact pair is the minimum a shop needs before handing an order to a delivery person, while still letting a customer place a COD or credit order first. Keeping the code, HMAC and rate limits in the OTP module means the new purposes cannot drift from the login rules, and the purpose inside the HMAC makes a code useless for another flow. Clearing the password on an OTP takeover turns "I know this phone today" into "this account is mine", which is safer than leaving a password somebody else set. Refusing at the transition, before any write, means a blocked delivery can never leave a status log or a notice behind.
- **Known limits:** Credit and COD orders can still be placed before verification; only delivery is blocked, and the Phase 5 staff screens must show the verification state and the refusal. An OTP-only customer has no email, so they are never asked for one. An email code requested before an email change could confirm the new address: the admin clears the verification on change, but pending codes are not cancelled. Staff and superusers still cannot use the shop login (decision pending). There is no profile page yet. The `accounts` migration is pending: the owner runs `makemigrations accounts`.
